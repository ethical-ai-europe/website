# Deploy AI Confidently and Responsibly

**AI can help your business compete, innovate and serve customers better.** But with opportunity comes responsibility. The EU AI Act creates clear rules, and understanding them gives you a competitive advantage.

This guide cuts through the complexity. Whether you're using AI tools, using AI in customer-facing services, or building AI into your products, you'll find practical guidance here.

> **What's new (November 2026):** The Digital Omnibus on AI (Regulation (EU) 2026/1744), in force since 27 July 2026, moved the high-risk rules (for example AI in hiring or credit scoring) to **2 December 2027**, from August 2026. Bans, AI literacy and transparency rules already apply. **The deadline moved, the direction didn't.** Use the time to prepare.

---

## Your Concerns Are Valid

### "What are my legal obligations?"
It depends on how you use AI. Every business using AI must avoid banned practices and take measures to support its staff's AI literacy. If AI talks to your customers or creates content they see, transparency rules apply too. Using AI for decisions about people, like hiring, brings much more from December 2027.

### "How do I know if my AI use is high-risk?"
The EU AI Act uses a risk-based approach. Most SMEs have light obligations, but some uses, like AI in hiring or credit decisions, are high-risk. The Commission published draft guidelines on classifying high-risk AI in May 2026; the final version is still pending.

### "We want to use AI but don't have a legal team."
You don't need one for most AI uses. Our practical guides explain what you need to do in plain language.

### "What's the timeline for compliance?"
Some requirements are already in effect; the high-risk rules arrive in December 2027 and August 2028. See the table below and the full [EU AI Act timeline](/en/timeline).

## What Applies Right Now

| Since | Rule | What it means for your business |
|-------|------|--------------------------------|
| **February 2025** | Prohibited practices | No manipulative AI, no social scoring, no emotion recognition of employees or job candidates |
| **February 2025, reworded July 2026** | AI literacy (Article 4) | Take measures to support staff's AI literacy: training, guidelines, a go-to person. No guaranteed level required. Applies to all businesses using AI |
| **2 August 2026** | Transparency (Article 50) | Chatbots must say they are AI; deepfakes and AI-generated public-interest text must be labelled; people must be told about emotion recognition or biometric categorisation |
| **2 December 2026** | New ban | No AI that creates intimate images of real people without consent. Providers of generative AI already on the market must have machine-readable marking in place |

Learn more about [AI transparency](/en/rights/ai-transparency) and the [ban on intimate deepfakes](/en/rights/intimate-deepfakes).

## Common Business AI Use Cases and Their Regulatory Status

Understanding how the EU AI Act treats different use cases helps you focus effort where it matters:

| Use Case | Risk Level | Key Requirements | Applies From |
|----------|------------|------------------|--------------|
| AI chatbot on website | Transparency | Tell users they're talking to AI | 2 Aug 2026 |
| Email spam filters, fraud detection | Minimal | AI literacy; good practice | Now |
| AI in hiring and CV screening | **High risk** | Full high-risk deployer duties apply from that date: use the system as the provider instructs, assign trained people to oversee it, monitor it and report serious problems, keep its logs for at least six months, inform workers' representatives and staff before using it at work, and tell people when it is used to make decisions about them | 2 Dec 2027 |
| Emotion analysis of job candidates or staff | **Prohibited** | Do not use (except medical/safety) | Feb 2025 |
| Credit scoring for customers | **High risk** | Full deployer duties, including a fundamental rights impact assessment (required for all deployers of credit scoring of individuals) | 2 Dec 2027 |
| AI for content recommendation | Minimal (usually) | Depends on platform and context | Now |
| AI-generated images or video in marketing | Transparency | Label deepfakes (realistic content of real people, places or events) | 2 Aug 2026 |
| Selling a generative AI product | Transparency (provider) | Machine-readable marking of output | 2 Aug 2026 (existing systems: 2 Dec 2026) |
| Biometric identification | **Prohibited/High risk** | Strict limits or ban | Bans now; high-risk 2 Dec 2027 |
| AI inside a regulated product (e.g. medical device, toy) | **High risk** | Conformity assessment (a check, by the provider or an independent body, that the system meets the rules), together with the product | 2 Aug 2028 |

*This is a general guide. Your use case may also be covered by sector-specific rules (financial services, healthcare and so on), and the GDPR always applies to personal data.*

## Penalties for Non-Compliance

The EU AI Act sets maximum fines (Article 99):

- **Prohibited AI practices:** up to €35 million or 7% of worldwide annual turnover
- **Most other obligations, including transparency:** up to €15 million or 3% of worldwide annual turnover
- **Supplying incorrect, incomplete or misleading information to authorities:** up to €7.5 million or 1% of worldwide annual turnover

For large companies the higher of the two amounts is the ceiling. For **SMEs** (including start-ups), the **lower** amount applies to every fine. For **small mid-caps** (a new category of companies just above SME size, created in 2026), the lower amount applies too, except for fines for banned practices. National authorities have been able to enforce since 2 August 2026 (the EU AI Office supervises general-purpose AI providers and certain large platforms), although many Member States were slow to set up their authorities.

**The earlier you start, the lower your compliance cost.** Building documentation, governance and oversight into your processes now is far cheaper than retrofitting them under deadline pressure in 2027.

## Three Steps to Start Now

### 1. Create an AI Inventory
Make a simple list: What AI tools and systems does your business use? For each one, note the purpose, vendor and data involved. Include AI features built into software you already use.

### 2. Classify Your Risk Levels
For each AI system, ask: Does it make or influence significant decisions about people? Is it in a high-risk area like employment or credit? Does it talk to customers or produce content they see? Use our [SME Guide](/en/for-organizations/sme-guide) to work out your risk level.

### 3. Address the Obvious Gaps
If AI talks to your customers, does it say it's AI? Have your staff had any AI training or guidance? If you use AI in hiring, does a human make every decision? Start with the obligations that already apply.

## Related Laws to Watch

- **Product Liability Directive (for products placed on the market from 9 December 2026):** the new EU product liability rules cover software, including AI. If you make or sell products with AI inside, review your liability position and supplier contracts.
- **Platform Work Directive (transposition due 2 December 2026):** if you run a digital labour platform, national rules will give workers rights on algorithmic management, including transparency and human review of automated decisions. Many countries are late, so check your national law.
- **GDPR:** applies in full to any AI that processes personal data. Proposed changes are still being negotiated; nothing in the GDPR has changed yet.

## The Business Case for Ethics

**Ethical AI isn't just about avoiding fines.** Organisations that use AI responsibly:

✅ **Build customer trust**: transparency about AI use strengthens relationships

✅ **Attract talent**: people want to work for responsible employers

✅ **Reduce risk**: proactive compliance prevents costly surprises

✅ **Improve decisions**: bias-tested, well-documented AI performs better

✅ **Future-proof the business**: early preparation means 2027 is not a scramble

**Ethics and good business go hand in hand.** Human oversight, clear documentation and trained people are good practice today, whatever the legal deadline.

## Why This Matters for Your Business

**The EU AI Act is the world's first comprehensive AI regulation.** It applies to any organisation offering or using AI systems that affect people in the EU, regardless of where the business is located.

For most SMEs using common AI tools, obligations are light: support AI literacy, be transparent with customers, and steer clear of banned uses. But if you use AI in hiring, credit decisions or other high-impact areas, take preparation seriously now, even though the full rules arrive in December 2027.

The good news: **The requirements aren't mysterious.** They come down to knowing what you're using, being transparent about it, keeping humans in the loop and documenting your approach.

Start now, start simple, and build from there. **The deadline moved, the direction didn't.**
