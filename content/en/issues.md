# Understanding the Issues

AI is reshaping society in ways that go far beyond what any single law or regulation can address. The EU AI Act sets important boundaries, but the deeper questions remain: How will AI change work? Who benefits from its productivity gains? What happens to democracy when algorithms shape what we see and believe? How do we preserve privacy in an age of ubiquitous surveillance?

None of this is predetermined. AI can be built and used to serve people, not only to maximise profit. Whether it does depends on human choices: by the people who design systems, the organisations that deploy them, the authorities that supervise them, and the citizens who speak up. The human factor is the key.

These questions don't have easy answers, and they need diverse voices to shape them.

## Where Things Stand in November 2026

The AI Act is being phased in step by step. Here is a short overview (see the [full timeline](/en/timeline) for details):

- **In force since February 2025:** bans on the most harmful practices, such as social scoring; manipulative AI or AI that exploits people's vulnerabilities in ways that cause or are likely to cause significant harm; untargeted scraping of facial images; emotion recognition at work and in schools; AI that sorts people by their biometric data (such as their face) to infer their race, political opinions, trade union membership, religion, sex life or sexual orientation; AI that predicts whether a specific person will commit a crime based solely on profiling or personality traits; and most real-time remote biometric identification by police in public spaces (with narrow exceptions). Organisations using AI must also support AI literacy among their staff.
- **In force since August 2025:** rules for providers of general-purpose AI models, such as the large models behind popular chatbots.
- **In force since 2 August 2026:** transparency duties, including telling people when they are talking to a chatbot and labelling deepfakes. National authorities and the EU AI Office can now supervise and enforce. [Read about your transparency rights →](/en/rights/ai-transparency)
- **From 2 December 2026:** a new ban on AI that creates sexually explicit images of real people without their consent. [Learn more about this new protection →](/en/rights/intimate-deepfakes)
- **Delayed:** the detailed rules for "high-risk" AI (used in hiring, credit, education, public services, policing and more) now apply from 2 December 2027, and from 2 August 2028 for AI built into products such as medical devices.

### The big change of 2026: the Digital Omnibus on AI

In July 2026, the EU adopted the Digital Omnibus on AI (Regulation (EU) 2026/1744). It postponed the high-risk rules by 12 to 16 months so that technical standards and guidance can be ready. Supporters say this gives organisations time to comply properly. Rights advocates worry that people affected by AI in hiring, credit or public services will wait longer for key protections. The Omnibus kept the bans and transparency duties in place and added the new ban on non-consensual intimate deepfakes.

Separately, proposed changes to the GDPR (the EU's data protection law) are still being negotiated. Nothing in the GDPR has changed yet.

## The Issues That Matter

### 🏭 Labor and Work

AI is transforming the workplace, from automating tasks to monitoring workers to reshaping entire industries. The anxiety is real, and so are the opportunities. AI can take over repetitive tasks and give people more time for work that needs human judgment, if workers have a say in how it is introduced.

What jobs are most exposed? What rights do workers have? How can we ensure the transition benefits everyone, not just those at the top?

[Explore AI and the Future of Work →](/en/issues/labor-market)

### 💰 Economic Impact

AI generates enormous value, but for whom? The benefits of AI are not distributed equally. Large tech companies with vast data resources and computing power have significant advantages, while smaller businesses and workers may struggle to capture their share.

Who wins and who loses in the AI economy? What policies can ensure broader prosperity?

[Explore Who Benefits from AI →](/en/issues/economic-impact)

### 🗳️ Democracy and Society

AI doesn't just affect individuals. It shapes our collective life. From the algorithms that curate our news feeds to deepfakes that erode trust, AI is transforming the information environment that democracy depends on.

How do we govern systems that increasingly govern us? How do we maintain democratic accountability?

[Explore AI, Democracy, and Society →](/en/issues/democracy-society)

### 👁️ Surveillance and Privacy

AI supercharges surveillance capabilities, enabling monitoring at a scale and precision previously impossible. Biometric identification, behavioral prediction, and pervasive tracking raise fundamental questions about freedom and privacy.

What are the limits? What protections exist? What can you do?

[Explore Surveillance, Privacy, and AI →](/en/issues/surveillance-privacy)
