# AI Affects Your Family. Know Your Rights.

**Whether AI is making decisions about your job, your children's education, or your access to services, you deserve to understand what's happening. And you have the power to do something about it.**

AI can genuinely help people: faster answers from public services, better learning support, fewer boring forms. Whether it does depends on the people who build it, buy it and question it. You are one of those people. You don't need to be an engineer or a lawyer to have a say.

You're not alone in wondering about these things. Many people share the same concerns.

---

## Your Concerns Are Valid

### "Is AI watching me at work?"
Workplace monitoring is increasingly common, but there are limits. Since February 2025, AI that tries to read your emotions at work (including during recruitment) is banned in the EU, except for medical or safety reasons.

### "What about my children's school?"
AI is entering classrooms through learning apps, plagiarism detection and student monitoring. Parents can ask what's being used and have the right to know what data is processed about their child. Emotion recognition in schools is already banned.

### "Can I trust AI decisions about me?"
When a decision about your loan, your job application or your benefits is made solely by an automated system and has a serious effect on you, the GDPR gives you the right to a meaningful explanation and, in many cases, to have a person look at it again. From 2 December 2027, the AI Act adds a right to be told when high-risk AI is used in decisions about you, and to an explanation of its role.

### "Is that image or chatbot real?"
Since August 2026, chatbots must tell you they are AI, and deepfakes must be labelled. From 2 December 2026, apps that create fake nude images of real people without consent are banned.

### "What happens to my data?"
European data protection law (GDPR) gives you control over how your personal data is used, including when AI is involved. It applies alongside the AI Act.

## What Protects You Today (November 2026)

- **Banned AI practices (since 2 February 2025).** Things like social scoring, manipulative AI that exploits vulnerable people, and emotion recognition at work and in schools are not allowed.
- **Transparency rules (since 2 August 2026).** You must be told when you are talking to a chatbot. Deepfakes and AI-generated text published to inform the public on matters of public interest must be labelled. If a system recognises emotions or sorts people by biometric traits, you must be informed. [Learn more about AI transparency →](/en/rights/ai-transparency)
- **Enforcement (since 2 August 2026).** National authorities and the EU AI Office now have powers to supervise and enforce the AI Act. In practice, some countries were late setting up their authorities, so enforcement is still uneven.
- **Your GDPR rights.** The GDPR protects you from decisions made solely by automated systems that have legal or similarly serious effects on you. In February 2025 the EU Court of Justice (case C-203/22, Dun & Bradstreet Austria) confirmed that for such decisions you have a right to a meaningful explanation of how the decision was reached, so you can understand and contest it. Simply handing over a complex algorithm is not enough.

## What's Coming

| Date | What changes for you |
|------|----------------------|
| **2 December 2026** | Ban on AI that creates realistic intimate or sexual images of identifiable people without consent, including child sexual abuse material ("nudifier" apps). EU countries should also have national laws giving platform workers (delivery riders, ride-hail drivers) rights on algorithmic management: transparency, human review of automated decisions and limits on data use. Many countries are late. |
| **2 December 2027** | Strict rules for high-risk AI in areas like education, hiring, credit scoring, public benefits, law enforcement and migration. |
| **2 August 2028** | High-risk rules for AI built into products such as medical devices, toys and vehicles. |
| **2 August 2030** | Deadline for high-risk AI systems that public authorities were already using to comply. |

[See the full timeline →](/en/timeline)

### Why were some dates moved?

The "Digital Omnibus" law, in force since 27 July 2026, pushed back the high-risk rules (originally August 2026) to December 2027 and August 2028. The stated reason is that the technical standards and guidance companies need were not ready. Supporters say this gives time to do it properly; critics worry it delays protections people were promised. Both concerns are fair. The important point for you: **the bans, the transparency rules and your GDPR rights are not delayed.** The same law also added the new ban on non-consensual intimate deepfakes.

### 👶 Children and AI: What Parents Need to Know

AI affects children in ways that deserve special attention, and European law gives children extra protections.

#### In the Classroom
Schools and educational platforms increasingly use AI for adaptive learning, automated grading, plagiarism detection and even attention monitoring. Some protections already apply: **emotion recognition in education has been banned since February 2025**, and schools that use AI must take steps to build AI literacy among their staff. AI used to admit pupils or evaluate their learning will count as **high-risk** from **2 December 2027**, with stricter safeguards. Until then, GDPR and good practice still apply. As a parent, you have the right to ask:
- Which AI tools does the school use?
- What data is collected about my child, and who has access?
- How are AI-generated assessments reviewed by teachers?

#### Deepfakes and "Nudifier" Apps
Fake nude images of classmates are a real and growing harm. From **2 December 2026**, AI systems that generate such images of identifiable people without consent are banned in the EU. Talk to your children about it, and know that this is never their fault if they are targeted.

[Intimate Deepfakes: what you can do →](/en/rights/intimate-deepfakes)

#### Content Recommendations
Social media and video platforms use recommendation algorithms designed mainly for engagement, not for children's wellbeing. Check platform settings and use parental controls where available.

#### Children's Data
Children's personal data gets extra protection under the GDPR. Platforms must have a lawful basis to process it, and data minimisation rules apply.

## Why This Matters for You

**AI isn't a future problem. It's a today problem.** Algorithms are already making decisions that affect your job, your children's education, your credit and your access to services.

But AI can also serve people well, when humans stay in charge of how it is used. **European law gives you real tools**: bans on the most harmful practices, the right to know when you're dealing with a chatbot or a deepfake, and, for significant automated decisions, the right to an explanation, to human review and to challenge the outcome.

You don't need to be a tech expert. You just need to know what questions to ask and where to find help. **That's why we're here.**

---

## Your Next Steps

1. **Ask one question.** Next time AI might be involved in a decision about you, ask: "Was any automated system involved in this decision, and can you explain how it reached its result?"
2. **Check your children's school.** Ask what AI tools are used, what data is collected and how long it's kept.
3. **Talk about deepfakes at home.** Before the ban starts on 2 December 2026, make sure your children know they can come to you.
4. **Know where to complain.** Find your national data protection authority on our [Resources page](/en/resources).
5. **Learn your rights.** Start with the [AI Rights overview](/en/rights) and the [timeline](/en/timeline).
