## What the Law Says Right Now

While the full story is being prepared, here is where things stand for anyone who has been screened out by an algorithm:

- **You can ask for an explanation and a human review today.** Under the GDPR, when a decision with serious effects on you is made entirely by an automated system, you generally have the right to be told, to get a meaningful explanation of the logic involved, and in many cases to have a person look at your case. Even if AI only influenced the decision, you can ask, and you always have the right to see the personal data used about you. In February 2025 the EU's highest court confirmed (in the Dun & Bradstreet Austria case) that the explanation must be one you can actually understand and use to contest the decision.
- **Emotion-reading AI in job interviews is banned.** Since 2 February 2025, AI that claims to read your emotions from your face or voice is prohibited at work and in recruitment.
- **You must be told when you're talking to a bot.** Since 2 August 2026, chatbots, including recruitment chatbots, must make clear that they are AI.
- **Stricter rules for hiring AI are on the way.** AI used to screen, rank or evaluate job candidates counts as "high-risk" under the AI Act. Its specific obligations (bias testing, human oversight, documentation) apply from **2 December 2027**, later than first planned.

Behind every automated rejection is a real person, and asking "was this decided by a machine?" is often the first step to a fairer outcome.
