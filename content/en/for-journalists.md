# Hold AI to Account

**AI is transforming society, and the public deserves to understand how.** As a journalist or activist, you play a crucial role in investigating AI systems, telling human stories and ensuring accountability.

AI can serve people, not just profits, but only if someone checks. You don't need to be an engineer to ask good questions about an algorithm. The most important questions are human ones: who is affected, who decided, and who answers for it.

This guide helps you navigate the AI landscape in Europe: understand where the rules stand in November 2026, find story angles, access reliable sources and connect with experts.

## Where Things Stand (November 2026)

| Date | What happened / happens | Status |
|------|-------------------------|--------|
| **2 February 2025** | Prohibited AI practices banned; AI literacy duty | In force |
| **2 August 2025** | General-purpose AI (GPAI) model rules; AI Office and penalties regime; deadline for countries to designate national authorities | In force |
| **27 July 2026** | Digital Omnibus on AI (Regulation (EU) 2026/1744) in force | In force |
| **2 August 2026** | Transparency duties (chatbots, deepfakes, AI-generated public-interest text); national authorities and AI Office can enforce; Commission can fine GPAI providers | In force |
| **2 December 2026** | New ban on non-consensual intimate deepfakes and AI-generated child sexual abuse material | Coming in weeks |
| **2 December 2027** | High-risk rules for Annex III areas, the list of high-risk areas in the law (hiring, education, credit, benefits, policing, migration, justice) | Upcoming (was Aug 2026) |
| **2 August 2028** | High-risk rules for AI in regulated products | Upcoming (was Aug 2027) |
| **2 August 2030** | Deadline for public authorities' existing high-risk systems | Upcoming |

**Note for fact-checking:** older articles saying "high-risk rules apply from August 2026" or "full application in August 2027" are now out of date. [Full timeline →](/en/timeline)

### The Omnibus delay, explained fairly
The Commission proposed the Digital Omnibus on AI in November 2025; it was agreed in May 2026 and entered into force on 27 July 2026. It postponed high-risk obligations because harmonised standards and guidance were not ready (the first harmonised standard, EN 18286 on quality management, was only cited in the Official Journal in October 2026). It also rewrote the AI literacy duty, created a legal basis to use sensitive data strictly for bias detection, gave the AI Office direct supervision of some systems, and added a new ban on non-consensual intimate deepfakes. Supporters call it pragmatic; critics call it a delay of promised protections. Both sides deserve a hearing.

## Story Angles Now That Enforcement Is Live

### Who is actually enforcing?
Enforcement powers have applied since 2 August 2026, but as of mid-June 2026 only 9 of 27 Member States had designated both their market surveillance authorities and their notifying authorities (the bodies that approve the independent testers). **Ask: who is the AI Act authority in your country, is it staffed and funded, and has it opened any investigations?** Denmark is a useful comparison: its law (in force since August 2025) names the Agency for Digital Government as the main authority, with the Data Protection Agency covering certain bans.

### Are the bans being respected?
Emotion recognition at work and in schools, social scoring and other practices have been banned since February 2025. Are vendors still selling "engagement" or "sentiment" tools to employers or schools? **Ask: what complaints have authorities received, and what did they do?** Be precise: no AI Act fine has been publicly confirmed so far, so don't report one without a primary source.

### Is AI-generated content labelled?
Since 2 August 2026, deepfakes must be labelled and AI-generated text published to inform the public on matters of public interest must be disclosed. The Commission published guidelines on these duties in July 2026. **Ask: are platforms, political campaigns and publishers (including newsrooms) complying?** Check how your own newsroom applies the guidelines. Note the exemption: AI-assisted text that has had human editorial review, under someone's editorial responsibility, does not need a label. Deepfakes in your reporting still do.

### General-purpose AI and the Code of Practice
The GPAI Code of Practice was published in July 2025. Most major providers signed it; Meta is not a signatory. Since 2 August 2026, the Commission can fine GPAI providers. **Ask: how is the AI Office supervising non-signatories, and what information has it requested?**

### Intimate deepfakes and "nudifier" apps
The ban starts on 2 December 2026. **Ask: are these apps still available in EU app stores after that date? Who is responsible for taking them down?**

### Platform workers and algorithmic management
EU countries must transpose the Platform Work Directive by 2 December 2026, giving delivery and ride-hail workers rights on transparency and human review of automated decisions. **Ask: has your country passed its law on time?** Many are late.

### Is GDPR about to change?
The data/GDPR part of the Digital Omnibus, including proposed changes to rules on automated decisions (Art. 22), is still under negotiation: the Council took its position in October 2026 and Parliament has not yet adopted its position. Nothing in GDPR has changed yet. Meanwhile the Court of Justice confirmed in February 2025 (C-203/22) that people have a right to a meaningful explanation of decisions made solely by automated means with significant effects. **Ask: would the proposals weaken this?**

### Workplace, hiring, public services, education and facial recognition
These remain the places where AI decisions hit hardest. The high-risk rules for them now start on 2 December 2027, and public bodies' existing systems have until 2 August 2030. **Ask: what are organisations doing in the meantime?**

### Who pays when AI causes harm?
The Commission withdrew its proposed AI Liability Directive (announced February 2025, formally withdrawn October 2025). The new Product Liability Directive, which covers software including AI, applies for products placed on the market from 9 December 2026. A good explainer angle for readers who are harmed.

### 🔍 How to Investigate AI Systems

**Freedom of information and access requests**
Public sector AI may be subject to access-to-information laws. In many Member States you can request:
- Details of AI systems used in government decision-making
- Impact assessments and risk registers
- Procurement contracts with AI vendors
- Audit logs and accuracy statistics
- Which authority supervises the AI Act locally, and its budget and staffing

**Technical approaches**
- **Algorithmic auditing:** test a system with affected communities by submitting varied inputs and documenting outputs
- **Crowdsourced data collection:** coordinate affected individuals to document their experiences
- **Public registers:** the AI Act requires registration of high-risk systems in an EU database; Annex III systems that providers judge not high-risk must also be registered. Watch these entries as the 2027 deadline approaches. Note: entries for law enforcement, migration and border systems go into a non-public section of the EU database, so they will not be visible to you.
- **GDPR access requests:** affected people can ask for a meaningful explanation of significant decisions made solely by automated means

**Interviewing affected communities**
The best AI stories are human stories. Find the people denied benefits, screened out in hiring, flagged by fraud detection or monitored at work.

## Why This Matters

**AI systems increasingly make decisions that affect millions of people, often with little transparency.** Rules only matter if people know they exist and if they're enforced. With enforcement powers now live but national authorities still patchy, this is the moment when public scrutiny counts most.

Journalists and activists serve as a crucial check on power. You **investigate**, **amplify** affected voices, **translate** complex rules and **hold** deployers and developers to account.
