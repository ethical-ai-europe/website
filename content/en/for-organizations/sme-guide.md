# AI Guide for Small & Medium Businesses

**AI can help your business compete, innovate and serve customers better.** But like any powerful tool, it comes with responsibilities. This guide helps SME owners and managers understand what the EU AI Act means for your business, in plain language, with practical steps.

You don't need a legal team or a dedicated compliance department. You need to understand what you're using and take reasonable steps to use it responsibly.

> **Update, November 2026:** The Digital Omnibus on AI (Regulation (EU) 2026/1744), in force since 27 July 2026, moved the rules for high-risk AI, such as AI in hiring or credit decisions, to **2 December 2027** (previously August 2026). Some rules already apply today: no banned AI, AI literacy, and transparency for chatbots and AI-generated content. **The deadline moved, the direction didn't.** Use the extra time to prepare calmly.

*This guide is not legal advice. It gives practical steps; for your specific situation, ask a qualified professional.*

---

## What Applies to Every SME Today

Whatever AI you use, these apply now:

1. **No prohibited AI.** For example, no AI that reads employees' or job candidates' emotions (banned at work since February 2025, except for medical or safety reasons).
2. **AI literacy.** You must *take measures to support* AI literacy among your staff. That means practical steps like a short training session, written guidelines on which tools to use and how, and a person staff can ask. You don't have to guarantee any particular level of expertise.
3. **Transparency** (since 2 August 2026) if you use AI with customers or the public: tell people when they are talking to a chatbot, label deepfakes, and label AI-generated text published to inform the public on matters of public interest (unless a person has reviewed it and takes editorial responsibility). Ordinary marketing copy is generally not covered.
4. **From 2 December 2026:** a new ban on AI that creates intimate or sexually explicit images of real people without consent. Make sure no tool you use or offer can be used for this.

## Am I Affected? A Quick Assessment

Your obligations depend on *how* you use AI. Two words help: a **provider** builds an AI system or sells it under its own name; a **deployer** uses one in its work. Most SMEs are deployers.

### 🟢 Using AI Tools for Internal Tasks
*Examples: AI assistants for drafting emails or research, automated scheduling*

**Your obligations:** Light
- Take AI literacy measures: brief staff on how to use the tools and their limits
- Don't share sensitive or confidential data inappropriately
- Follow the tool provider's terms of service
- Tell employees which AI tools are in use and how

### 🟡 Using AI in Customer-Facing Systems
*Examples: AI chatbot on your website, AI-powered customer service, AI-generated images or videos in marketing*

**Your obligations:** Moderate, and they apply now
- Tell customers when they're interacting with AI (since 2 August 2026)
- Label deepfakes (realistic AI-generated or altered images, audio or video)
- Check AI-generated content for accuracy
- Have a process for handling complaints
- Document what systems you're using

### 🟠 Using AI for Significant Decisions
*Examples: AI-assisted CV screening, credit decisions, employee performance evaluation*

**Your obligations:** Significant. Likely "high-risk" from **2 December 2027**
- Full high-risk deployer duties apply from that date: use the system as the provider instructs, assign trained people to oversee it, monitor it and report serious problems, keep its logs for at least six months, inform workers' representatives and staff before using it at work, and tell people when it is used to make decisions about them; for credit scoring or life/health insurance pricing, a fundamental rights impact assessment
- Start preparing now: ask your vendor for documentation and keep a human in charge of every decision
- [See Compliance Checklist →](/en/for-organizations/compliance-checklist)

### 🔴 Building AI Products or Features
*Examples: building AI into your product, training custom models, creating AI-powered services*

**Your obligations:** Extensive
- You may be a provider, with documentation and monitoring duties
- If your product generates images, audio, video or text, its output must be marked as AI-generated in a machine-readable way (systems already on the market before 2 August 2026: by 2 December 2026)
- High-risk products need a conformity assessment (a check, by the provider or an independent body, that the system meets the rules). This applies from 2 December 2027, or 2 August 2028 for AI in regulated products like medical devices or toys
- Consider consulting an expert

## Understanding Risk Categories

The EU AI Act uses a risk-based approach. Knowing where your AI use falls tells you what's required, and when.

### Minimal Risk ✅
Most AI applications fall here. Think:
- Spam filters
- AI-powered search
- Recommendation engines
- General productivity tools

**What's required:** AI literacy measures and good practice. No other specific obligations, but responsible use is always good business.

### Transparency ("Limited") Risk ⚠️
AI systems with disclosure duties, in force since 2 August 2026:
- Chatbots (people must know they're not talking to a human)
- Emotion recognition and biometric categorisation (people exposed to it must be told; and emotion recognition at work is banned)
- Deepfakes, and AI-generated text published to inform the public on matters of public interest

**What's required:** Disclosure and labelling. Read more in [AI transparency: your rights](/en/rights/ai-transparency).

### High Risk 🔶
AI in sensitive areas with significant impact on people. Rules apply from **2 December 2027**:
- **Employment:** AI in recruiting, hiring, performance evaluation
- **Credit:** AI for creditworthiness or loan decisions
- **Essential services:** access to public benefits, creditworthiness checks, life and health insurance pricing, and emergency call triage
- **Education:** determining access or outcomes (less relevant for most SMEs)

**What's required (from December 2027):** risk management, documentation, human oversight, testing and registration (mostly for providers), plus deployer duties like oversight and monitoring. [See the full checklist →](/en/for-organizations/compliance-checklist)

### Prohibited ❌
Certain AI uses are banned entirely (since February 2025 unless stated):
- Manipulative AI designed to distort behaviour
- Exploiting vulnerabilities (age, disability, social or economic situation)
- Social scoring systems
- Emotion recognition at work or in education (except medical or safety reasons)
- Real-time biometric identification in public by police (with narrow exceptions)
- **From 2 December 2026:** AI that generates intimate images of real people without consent ([learn more](/en/rights/intimate-deepfakes))

**What's required:** Don't do it. If a vendor offers you something that sounds like this, walk away.

## 5 Practical Steps for SMEs

### 1. Inventory Your AI Use 📋

You can't manage what you don't know about. Create a simple list:

| AI System/Tool | Purpose | Data Involved | Vendor | Risk Level |
|----------------|---------|---------------|--------|------------|
| *Example: AI writing assistant* | *Email drafting* | *No personal data* | *Vendor A* | *Minimal* |
| *Example: Website chatbot* | *Customer questions* | *Customer queries* | *Vendor B* | *Transparency* |
| *Example: CV screening tool* | *CV screening* | *Applicant data* | *Vendor C* | *High (from Dec 2027)* |

Start simple. Don't forget AI features built into software you already use. You can add detail later.

### 2. Classify Risk Levels 🎯

For each system, ask:
- Does this AI make or influence significant decisions about people?
- Could someone be meaningfully harmed by a wrong output?
- Does it involve sensitive personal data?
- Is it in a regulated area (employment, credit, essential services)?
- Does it talk to customers or create content they will see?

If you answer "yes" to any of these, you likely need more than the basics. The Commission published draft guidelines on classifying high-risk AI in May 2026; the final version is still pending.

### 3. Document What You're Using and Why 📝

Good documentation doesn't have to be complicated. For each AI system, record:
- What it does
- What data it processes
- Who the vendor is
- Why you chose it
- When you started using it
- Who's responsible for it

This protects you and helps you answer questions from customers, staff or authorities.

### 4. Ensure Human Oversight 👤

For any AI that affects people meaningfully:
- A human should review important decisions
- Staff should be trained to understand the AI's limitations (this is also part of your AI literacy duty)
- There should be a process to override or escalate AI outputs
- Someone should be responsible for monitoring performance

This is good practice now and will be a legal requirement for high-risk uses from December 2027.

### 5. Be Transparent 🔍

Good transparency is simple:
- Tell customers when they're interacting with AI (required since 2 August 2026)
- Label AI-generated images, video or audio that could be mistaken for real
- Explain when AI plays a role in decisions that affect them
- Be honest about what AI can and can't do
- Respond to questions about your AI use

## Common Scenarios: What Do I Do?

### "We use an AI assistant to draft customer emails"

**Risk level:** Minimal

**What to do:**
- Consider telling customers that emails are AI-assisted (the AI Act generally does not require this for ordinary business emails, but it builds trust)
- Review AI outputs before sending, especially for complex matters
- Don't put confidential customer data into tools without checking the data terms
- Give staff short guidelines on appropriate use (this counts towards AI literacy)

### "We bought an AI hiring tool"

**Risk level:** HIGH (full rules from 2 December 2027)

**What to do now:**
- Make sure the tool does not analyse candidates' emotions, for example in video interviews. That is already banned.
- Get documentation from the vendor about its AI Act plans, bias testing and accuracy
- Ensure a human reviews every hiring decision; never let the AI decide alone
- Tell candidates that AI is used in the process
- Inform staff representatives (e.g. works council) before introducing the tool; required for high-risk workplace AI from December 2027 and often already under national labour law
- Document your use and decision-making process
- Check GDPR rules on automated decisions (decisions that are solely automated, with legal or similarly significant effects), which apply today
- Consider whether you actually need this tool
- [Review full requirements →](/en/for-organizations/compliance-checklist) and [AI in hiring](/en/daily-life/hiring)

### "We want to add a chatbot to our website"

**Risk level:** Transparency obligations (apply now)

**What to do:**
- Clearly tell users they're talking to an AI
- Provide an easy way to reach a human
- Monitor conversations for issues
- Feed the bot accurate information
- Have a plan for when the bot gives wrong answers

### "We're building an AI feature into our product"

**Risk level:** Varies, potentially significant

**What to do:**
- Assess the risk level based on use case and impact
- If it generates content, plan machine-readable marking of the output
- If high-risk, full requirements apply from December 2027 (or August 2028 for regulated products)
- Note the new Product Liability Directive, which covers software including AI, applies from 9 December 2026
- Document your development process
- Build in monitoring and feedback mechanisms
- Consider a national regulatory sandbox (SMEs get priority access; every Member State must have one by 2 August 2027)
- You may want to consult an AI compliance expert

## What If We Get It Wrong? Fines and Relief for SMEs

The AI Act sets maximum fines: up to €35 million or 7% of worldwide turnover for prohibited practices, up to €15 million or 3% for most other breaches (including transparency), and up to €7.5 million or 1% for giving authorities incorrect information. For SMEs (including start-ups), the **lower** of the two amounts applies, not the higher. For "small mid-caps" (a new category of companies just above SME size), the lower amount applies too, except for fines for prohibited practices.

National authorities have been able to enforce since 2 August 2026 (the EU AI Office supervises general-purpose AI providers and certain large platforms). The best protection is simple: no banned uses, clear disclosure, trained staff and humans in charge of important decisions.

- **Chamber of Commerce and industry associations**: many offer SME-focused AI guidance

### Questions to Ask Your Vendors
If you use AI from third parties, ask:
1. How is this AI classified under the EU AI Act, and does it have transparency features (disclosure, labelling)?
2. What is your plan for meeting high-risk requirements by December 2027 (if relevant)?
3. What documentation can you provide about testing and accuracy?
4. How do you test for and reduce bias?
5. What data does the system collect and process, and where?
6. What happens if the AI makes a mistake, and who is liable?
7. Can we audit or review how the system works?

### When to Get Professional Help
Consider consulting an expert if:
- You use or plan to use high-risk AI
- You're building AI into your products
- You're in a regulated industry
- You've received a complaint about your AI
- You're unsure about your obligations

## Timeline: What's Required When?

| Date | What Happens |
|------|--------------|
| **February 2025** | Prohibited AI practices banned; AI literacy duty |
| **August 2025** | Rules for general-purpose AI models (affects the big model providers) |
| **27 July 2026** | AI literacy rule rewritten: organisations must take measures to support AI literacy |
| **2 August 2026** | Transparency rules: chatbot disclosure, labelling of deepfakes |
| **2 December 2026** | New ban on non-consensual intimate deepfakes; marking deadline for existing generative AI |
| **2 August 2027** | Regulatory sandboxes in every Member State (priority for SMEs) |
| **2 December 2027** | High-risk rules (hiring, credit, essential services, education) |
| **2 August 2028** | High-risk rules for AI in regulated products |

See the full [EU AI Act timeline](/en/timeline).

### Your SME Action Plan

**Now:** make your AI inventory; brief staff on AI use (AI literacy); disclose chatbots and label AI-generated media; stop any banned use.

**By 2 December 2026:** check no tool you use or sell can create intimate images of real people without consent; if you sell generative AI, add machine-readable marking; review product liability terms.

**Before 2 December 2027:** for hiring, credit or similar AI, get vendor documentation, set up human oversight and monitoring, and inform affected people.

**By 2 August 2028:** if your product is a regulated product with AI inside (e.g. a medical device or toy), complete the high-risk requirements.

**Our advice:** Don't wait. The organisations that prepare early will have the smoothest transitions.

## Key Takeaways

✅ **Most SMEs using common AI tools have light obligations**: AI literacy, no banned uses, transparency where relevant

✅ **Know what you're using**: an AI inventory is your foundation

✅ **Be honest about risk levels**, especially for HR and decision-making AI

✅ **Keep humans in the loop**, especially for important decisions

✅ **Be transparent**: disclosure is required now and builds trust

✅ **Document as you go**: it's easier than catching up later

✅ **Use the delay well**: the deadline moved, the direction didn't
