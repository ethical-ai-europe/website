# AI Guidance for Educators & Schools

**AI in education isn't just a technical decision. It's a choice about how we shape young minds.** When algorithms help decide what students learn, how they're assessed or what opportunities they get, we need to apply special care.

This guide helps teachers, school leaders and education policymakers use AI responsibly. It covers both the requirements of the EU AI Act and practical guidance for ethical use.

> **Update, November 2026:** The Digital Omnibus on AI (Regulation (EU) 2026/1744), in force since 27 July 2026, moved the detailed rules for high-risk AI in education to **2 December 2027** (previously August 2026). But several rules already apply to schools today, and a new ban on AI-generated intimate images of real people starts on **2 December 2026**. **The deadline moved, the direction didn't.**

*This guide is not legal advice. Check your specific situation with your school's legal adviser, data protection officer or education authority.*

---

## What Applies to Schools Right Now

- **Emotion recognition is banned in education** (since February 2025), except for medical or safety reasons. No AI that claims to detect whether students are bored, attentive or stressed from their face or voice.
- **AI literacy.** Schools and universities using AI must *take measures to support* the AI literacy of their staff (rule rewritten in July 2026). There's no required level each person must reach, but staff training and clear guidance are expected.
- **Transparency** (since 2 August 2026). AI chatbots that students, parents or staff interact with must make clear they are AI. Deepfakes must be labelled.
- **New ban from 2 December 2026:** AI systems that generate realistic intimate or sexually explicit images of identifiable people without consent, including child sexual abuse material. This targets so-called "nudifier" apps, which can be misused against students and teachers. See [Intimate deepfakes](/en/rights/intimate-deepfakes).
- **GDPR** applies in full to all student data, today as always.

## Why Education AI Requires Special Care

Children and students deserve extra protection:

- **Developing minds** are more open to influence
- **Power imbalances** between institutions and students are significant
- **Long-term impacts** of educational decisions shape entire lives
- **Fairness matters more** when opportunities are at stake
- **Data sensitivity** is heightened for minors

The EU AI Act recognises this. **AI systems used in education are classified as high-risk** when they:
- Determine access or admission to education or training
- Evaluate learning outcomes
- Assess the appropriate level of education a student should receive
- Monitor and detect prohibited behaviour during tests (for example AI proctoring)

**When do the high-risk rules apply?** From **2 December 2027**. Public authorities (which can include public schools and education authorities) that already use such systems have until **2 August 2030** at the latest. Don't treat that as permission to wait: the safeguards below are good practice now, and procurement decisions you make today will still be in use in 2027.

This doesn't mean you can't use AI in education. It means you must use it thoughtfully.

## Framework for Evaluating AI Tools

Before adopting any AI tool, work through these five questions:

### 1. Purpose: What Problem Does It Solve?

- Is there a genuine educational need?
- Is AI the right solution, or are simpler alternatives available?
- What's the expected benefit to students?
- Could this be achieved without AI?

**Red flag:** AI tools searching for problems to solve, rather than addressing clear educational needs.

### 2. Data: What Does It Collect and Where Does It Go?

- What student data is collected?
- Where is the data stored? (EU vs. non-EU)
- How long is it retained?
- Who has access?
- Is it shared with third parties?
- Can data be deleted when requested?

**Red flag:** Vague answers about data handling, or data leaving EU jurisdiction without clear safeguards.

### 3. Bias: Has It Been Tested for Fairness?

- Has the system been tested for bias across different student groups?
- Are there performance differences based on language, disability or socioeconomic background?
- How diverse was the training data?
- What ongoing monitoring exists?

**Red flag:** No bias testing, or dismissive responses to fairness questions.

### 4. Transparency: Can Students and Parents Understand It?

- Can you explain to parents how the AI works?
- Will students understand why they received certain results?
- Is there documentation in accessible language?
- Can decisions be explained, not just announced?
- Does the tool tell users when they are talking to an AI?

**Red flag:** "Black box" systems that can't be explained to non-technical stakeholders.

### 5. Human Oversight: Who Reviews AI Decisions?

- Do teachers review AI recommendations before they affect students?
- Is there a process to override AI decisions?
- Who is accountable for AI outcomes?
- How do you catch and correct AI errors?

**Red flag:** Fully automated decisions with no human review.

## Policy Template: AI in Your School

Consider developing a school AI policy covering these elements:

### AI Tool Approval Process

- Who can propose new AI tools?
- What evaluation criteria must be met?
- Who approves adoption?
- How are existing tools reviewed?
- How do we check that a tool is not a banned use (such as emotion recognition)?

**Template language:**
> "All AI tools used for educational purposes must be approved by [designated authority] following evaluation against our AI assessment framework. No AI tool shall be used with students until approval is documented."

### Student Data Protection

- Compliance with GDPR and national data protection laws
- Data minimisation: collect only what's necessary
- Consent requirements for minors
- Parental notification procedures
- Data retention limits

**Template language:**
> "Student data processed by AI systems shall be limited to what is strictly necessary for the educational purpose. Parents/guardians must be informed of AI data processing, and appropriate consent obtained where required by law."

### Staff AI Literacy and Training

The AI Act requires organisations using AI to take measures to support their staff's AI literacy. In a school, that means:

- Basic AI literacy for all staff
- Specific training for teachers using AI tools
- Understanding of limitations and bias risks
- Ongoing professional development

**Template language:**
> "Staff using AI tools with students must complete approved training covering the tool's function, limitations, and appropriate use. This training must be refreshed [annually/when tools change]."

### Parent Communication

- Notification of AI use in education
- Plain-language explanations of AI systems
- Process for parents to ask questions
- Opt-out provisions where appropriate

**Template language:**
> "Parents/guardians shall be informed when AI systems are used in ways that may affect their child's education or generate records about their child. Information shall be provided in accessible language."

### Student AI Literacy

- Age-appropriate education about AI
- Understanding of AI limitations
- Skills for critical evaluation of AI outputs
- Rights awareness

**Template language:**
> "Students shall receive age-appropriate education about AI systems used in their learning, including how these systems work, their limitations, and students' rights regarding AI-assisted decisions."

### Image-Based Abuse and Deepfakes

AI-generated intimate images of students or staff are a safeguarding issue, and from 2 December 2026 the AI systems that create them without consent are banned in the EU. Your policy should cover:

- Clear rules that creating or sharing intimate or degrading deepfakes of anyone is serious misconduct
- How students and staff can report it safely and confidentially
- Support for the person targeted, and when to involve parents and the police
- Teaching students about consent and the harm deepfakes cause

**Template language:**
> "Creating, requesting or sharing sexualised or degrading AI-generated images of any student or staff member is prohibited. Incidents will be handled under our safeguarding procedures, with priority given to the wellbeing of the person affected."

## Generative AI in the Classroom

Generative AI tools such as AI chatbots present unique challenges for education. Here's practical guidance:

### Academic Integrity: Beyond Detection

"AI detection" tools are unreliable and can produce false positives, unfairly accusing students of misconduct. Instead of trying to catch AI use, consider these approaches:

**Redesign assessments:**
- Focus on process, not just final products
- Include in-class components
- Ask for personal reflection and connection to course discussions
- Use oral examinations or presentations
- Design assignments that require recent or local information

**Teach responsible use:**
- Make expectations explicit
- Discuss when AI assistance is and isn't appropriate
- Help students understand why learning the underlying skills matters
- Treat AI tools like other sources, requiring acknowledgment

**Evaluate understanding:**
- Ask follow-up questions
- Ask students to explain their reasoning
- Check for understanding in multiple ways
- Focus on demonstrated learning, not polished output

### Teaching WITH AI, Not Just Detecting It

Generative AI can enhance learning when used thoughtfully:

**Appropriate uses:**
- Brainstorming and idea generation
- Explaining concepts in different ways
- Providing practice problems with explanations
- Language support for non-native speakers
- Accessibility accommodations
- Helping students understand feedback

**Uses requiring caution:**
- Research (AI can produce false information)
- Grading or assessment (may become high-risk AI from December 2027)
- Personalisation based on sensitive characteristics
- Any use with younger children without supervision

### Age-Appropriate Use Policies

| Age Group | Guidance |
|-----------|----------|
| **Primary (5-11)** | Teacher-mediated AI use only; no independent student access to chatbots; focus on AI awareness |
| **Lower Secondary (11-14)** | Supervised use with clear guidelines; explicit instruction on limitations; closed-environment tools preferred |
| **Upper Secondary (14-18)** | Guided independent use; critical evaluation skills; responsible use agreements; academic integrity discussions |
| **Post-Secondary (18+)** | Adult-level expectations; clear course policies; discipline-specific guidance; preparation for workplace AI use |

## Questions to Ask Vendors

Before purchasing AI educational tools, require clear answers:

### Compliance Questions
1. How is your product classified under the EU AI Act? Is it high-risk?
2. If high-risk, what is your plan to meet the requirements by 2 December 2027, including conformity assessment (a check, by the provider or an independent body, that the system meets the rules)?
3. Can you provide technical documentation and instructions for use?
4. Does your product include any emotion recognition features? (These are banned in education.)
5. Does your chatbot tell users it is an AI, and is generated content marked as AI-generated?
6. How do you ensure GDPR compliance for student data?

### Fairness Questions
7. What bias testing has been conducted?
8. Can you share results of fairness evaluations?
9. How diverse was your training data?
10. What ongoing monitoring do you conduct?

### Transparency Questions
11. Can you explain how your AI reaches its outputs in plain language?
12. What documentation is available for parents and students?

### Data Questions
13. Exactly what student data do you collect?
14. Where is data stored? For how long?
15. Who has access to student data?
16. Is data used for any purpose beyond serving our students?
17. What happens to student data if we stop using your service?

### Accountability Questions
18. Who is responsible if the AI makes errors affecting students?
19. What's your process for handling complaints?
20. How can teachers override AI recommendations?
21. What insurance or liability coverage do you have?

**Vendor won't answer clearly?** That's a red flag. Consider alternatives.

## Phased Plan for Schools

**Now**
- List the AI tools in use across the school, including AI features inside existing platforms
- Remove any emotion recognition or "attention tracking" features
- Put staff AI literacy measures in place: training and a short written policy
- Check that chatbots used by students or parents identify themselves as AI

**By 2 December 2026**
- Update safeguarding and behaviour policies to cover AI-generated intimate images
- Make sure no school device or approved app offers "nudify" or similar features
- Teach students about consent, deepfakes and how to report abuse

**Before 2 December 2027**
- Identify AI used for admissions, grading, placement or exam proctoring (likely high-risk)
- Get documentation and instructions for use from vendors; build vendor compliance into contracts
- Set up human review of every AI-assisted decision about a student
- Public schools and education authorities: prepare a fundamental rights impact assessment (FRIA), using the template questionnaire the AI Office is to provide, and prepare registration of high-risk systems

**By 2 August 2030 (public education bodies)**
- High-risk systems already in use before the rules applied must comply. Aim to get there well before.

## Key Takeaways

✅ **Education AI is high-risk**: full rules from December 2027, but prepare now

✅ **Some rules apply today**: no emotion recognition, staff AI literacy, chatbot disclosure

✅ **Act on deepfakes before 2 December 2026**: update safeguarding policies

✅ **Evaluate before adopting**: use the five-question framework

✅ **Protect student data**: apply GDPR plus extra care for minors

✅ **Ensure human oversight**: teachers should review AI recommendations

✅ **Be transparent**: parents and students deserve to understand

✅ **Focus on learning, not detection**: redesign assessment for the AI age

✅ **Ask hard questions of vendors**: if they can't answer, reconsider
