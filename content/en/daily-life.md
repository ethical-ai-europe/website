# AI in Your Daily Life

**AI is already part of your daily life—often in ways you don't see.** When your child uses an adaptive learning app, when a doctor reviews your scan, when you apply for a job online, or when you interact with government services, artificial intelligence may be working behind the scenes.

This isn't science fiction. It's happening now, and you have rights.

## Where Things Stand (November 2026)

The EU AI Act is the European Union's law on artificial intelligence. It entered into force in 2024 and its rules apply in stages. In July 2026 the EU changed some of its dates (through an amending law often called the "Digital Omnibus on AI"). Here is the honest picture:

**Already protecting you today:**
- **Banned AI practices** (since February 2025). For example, AI that tries to read people's emotions is banned in schools and workplaces, including during recruitment (with narrow medical and safety exceptions). Social scoring and manipulative AI are banned too.
- **AI literacy** (since February 2025). Organisations that use AI must take steps to help their staff understand it.
- **Transparency** (since 2 August 2026). A chatbot must tell you that you are talking to an AI, and deepfakes must be labelled. See [When AI must tell you it's AI](/en/rights/ai-transparency).
- **Your GDPR rights.** When a decision with significant effects on you is made solely by a machine, the EU's data protection law gives you the right to be told, to get a meaningful explanation, and to ask for a human to review it. You can also always ask what personal data an organisation holds about you.

**Coming soon:**
- **2 December 2026:** A new ban on AI that creates fake sexual or intimate images of real people without their consent (so-called "nudifier" apps). See [Intimate deepfakes](/en/rights/intimate-deepfakes).

**Delayed:**
- The detailed rules for **"high-risk" AI** (AI used in areas like education, hiring, credit and public benefits) now apply from **2 December 2027**, not August 2026 as first planned. AI built into medical devices follows from **2 August 2028**. Public authorities have until **2 August 2030** for high-risk systems they already use. The EU says the delay gives time for technical standards to be ready; rights groups criticise it for postponing protections.

This delay means organisations have fewer formal AI Act duties right now. But it does not leave you unprotected: the bans, transparency rules, data protection and anti-discrimination law all apply today. And the most important safeguard is still a person. **When an automated decision matters to you, ask for a human.** See the full [timeline](/en/timeline).

## Questions to Ask in Any Context

When you suspect AI is involved in decisions that affect you, start with these questions:

**1. "Was AI or an automated system involved in this decision?"**
Ask directly. Under the GDPR, organisations must tell you when a decision with significant effects on you is made solely by automated means. Even when the law doesn't require them to tell you, you can still ask.

**2. "How did the system reach this conclusion?"**
For significant decisions made solely by a machine, you're entitled to a meaningful explanation, not just a result. In 2025 the EU Court of Justice confirmed that this means explaining the procedure and principles actually used in a way you can understand, so you can challenge it.

**3. "Can a human review this decision?"**
For significant decisions made solely by a machine, you have the right to ask for a person to look at your case. In other situations, asking is still worth it.

**4. "What data was used, and was it accurate?"**
Decisions are only as good as the data they're based on.

**5. "How can I challenge this if I disagree?"**
Know your options for appeal or complaint.

## Your Rights Apply Everywhere

Whether you're a student, patient, job seeker, or citizen interacting with government:

✓ **You have the right to know** when a significant decision about you is made solely by automated means (GDPR), and when you are chatting with an AI rather than a person (AI Act, since August 2026)

✓ **You have the right to explanation** of how significant, solely automated decisions are made

✓ **You have the right to human review** for significant decisions made solely by a machine

✓ **You can object and complain**: you can object to some uses of your personal data, complain to your data protection authority, and you have the right not to be discriminated against

The AI Act, the GDPR and equality law work together. You don't need to be a legal expert to use them, and asking for a human is always a reasonable request.
