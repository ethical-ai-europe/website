# AI Act timeline: what applies when

The EU AI Act does not switch on all at once. Different rules start on different dates between 2024 and 2030. In 2026 some of those dates were moved by an amending law, the Digital Omnibus on AI.

This page shows what applies today and what is still coming, in plain language.

**Last checked: 6 November 2026.** This is a summary, not legal advice. Dates come from the AI Act (Regulation (EU) 2024/1689) as amended by Regulation (EU) 2026/1744.

## At a glance

- **In force now:** the bans on harmful AI practices, AI literacy, rules for general-purpose AI models, transparency rules for chatbots and deepfakes, and supervision and enforcement.
- **Coming in weeks (2 December 2026):** a new ban on AI "nudifier" tools that create intimate images of real people without consent.
- **Coming later:** the main rules for high-risk AI in hiring, education, credit, benefits, policing and other sensitive areas (from December 2027), and for AI inside products such as medical devices (from August 2028).

## The timeline

### 1 August 2024: the AI Act enters into force

**Status: Done**

The law is published and becomes part of EU law. Nothing is required yet, but the clock starts for each phase below.

### 2 February 2025: first bans and AI literacy

**Status: In force now**

- Eight AI practices are banned in the EU. Examples include manipulative AI that causes significant harm, social scoring, and emotion recognition at work or in education (except for medical or safety reasons).
- Organisations that provide or use AI must take steps to build the AI literacy of their staff (the rule was reworded in July 2026, see below).

### 2 August 2025: general-purpose AI and governance

**Status: In force now**

- Providers of general-purpose AI models (the large models behind many chatbots and tools) must meet obligations such as documentation and a summary of training data.
- The EU-level governance is set up: the AI Office and the AI Board.
- The penalties regime is in place, and EU countries had to name their national authorities by this date. Many were late: by mid-June 2026 only 9 of 27 had named both of the required authorities.

### 27 July 2026: the Digital Omnibus on AI enters into force

**Status: In force now**

- A rewritten AI literacy rule (Article 4) applies.
- A new Article 4a allows organisations to process sensitive personal data strictly to detect and correct bias, with strong safeguards, including deleting the data afterwards.

### 2 August 2026: general application and transparency

**Status: In force now**

- **Transparency rules (Article 50)** apply. Chatbots must tell you that you are dealing with an AI, unless it is obvious from the context. Deepfakes, and AI-generated text published to inform the public on matters of public interest, must be labelled. People using emotion recognition or biometric categorisation must inform those exposed to it. See [your right to know when AI is involved](/en/rights/ai-transparency).
- The AI Office and national authorities can supervise and enforce the rules.
- The European Commission can fine providers of general-purpose AI models.

### 2 December 2026: new ban on intimate deepfakes

**Status: Coming in weeks**

- A ninth ban applies: AI systems that generate or manipulate realistic intimate or sexually explicit images of identifiable people without consent, including child sexual abuse material. This targets so-called "nudifier" apps. See [intimate deepfakes](/en/rights/intimate-deepfakes).
- Generative AI systems that were already on the market before 2 August 2026 must now also mark their output in a machine-readable way, so AI-generated content can be detected.

### 2 August 2027: regulatory sandboxes

**Status: Coming**

Each EU country must have at least one AI regulatory sandbox running: a supervised space where organisations can test AI with guidance from authorities. This was moved from 2026.

### 2 December 2027: high-risk rules for sensitive areas

**Status: Coming**

The main high-risk rules start for AI used in the Annex III areas (the list of high-risk areas in the law):

- biometrics
- critical infrastructure
- education and vocational training
- employment and HR, including hiring
- access to essential public and private services, including credit scoring and benefits
- law enforcement, migration and border control
- justice and democratic processes

This date was 2 August 2026 before the Digital Omnibus.

### 2 August 2028: high-risk rules for AI in products

**Status: Coming**

High-risk rules start for AI built into products that are already regulated in the EU (Annex I: AI built into products already covered by EU safety laws), such as medical devices, toys, lifts and vehicles. This date was 2 August 2027 before the Digital Omnibus.

### 2 August 2030: public authorities' existing systems

**Status: Coming**

High-risk AI systems that public authorities were already using must comply by this date.

## What changed in 2026: the Digital Omnibus

The Commission proposed the "Digital Omnibus on AI" on 19 November 2025. EU lawmakers reached a political agreement on 7 May 2026, the Council gave final approval on 29 June 2026, and the law (Regulation (EU) 2026/1744) entered into force on 27 July 2026.

### What it changed

- **High-risk deadlines were delayed.** Annex III high-risk rules moved from August 2026 to 2 December 2027, and rules for AI in products from August 2027 to 2 August 2028. The stated reason is to give time for technical standards and guidance to be ready. The first harmonised standard (on quality management) was cited in October 2026; others are still being written.
- **AI literacy was rewritten.** Organisations must take measures to support the AI literacy of their staff, but they do not have to guarantee a specific level for each person. It still applies to every organisation using AI, of any size.
- **A new ban was added** on AI that creates non-consensual intimate images or child sexual abuse material, from 2 December 2026.
- **Bias detection got a legal basis.** Sensitive personal data may be used strictly to find and fix bias, with safeguards.
- **The AI Office got more direct powers** over AI systems built on a provider's own general-purpose model and over AI in very large online platforms and search engines.
- **Smaller companies got lighter treatment.** SMEs get lower maximum fines, a new group called "small mid-caps" gets lower maximum fines except for banned practices, and both get priority access to sandboxes.
- **Registration still matters.** If a provider decides that a system in an Annex III area is not high-risk, it must still register it, with less information.
- **Help with rights assessments.** The AI Office will provide a template questionnaire for fundamental rights impact assessments.

### What it means for you as a citizen

- The bans and transparency rules you rely on today are not delayed. You should already be told when you are talking to a chatbot.
- You get a new protection against AI nudifier apps from 2 December 2026.
- The detailed AI Act safeguards for AI used in hiring, credit, benefits, education or policing arrive later than first planned. In the meantime, **GDPR still protects you**: you can ask for access to your data and, for decisions made solely by automated means that have legal or similarly significant effects, a meaningful explanation and human review. See the [right to explanation](/en/rights/right-to-explanation) and [algorithmic decisions](/en/rights/algorithmic-decisions).

Supporters of the delay say rules without finished standards would be hard to follow. Critics worry that people affected by high-risk AI wait longer for protection. Both points are worth keeping in mind.

### What it means for you as an organisation

- Extra time is not a reason to stop. AI literacy, the bans and transparency duties apply now.
- Use the time to map where you use AI, put real human oversight in place and prepare documentation. These steps make your AI better for the people it affects, not just compliant.
- Follow the Commission's guidance as it is finalised. Draft guidelines on classifying high-risk AI were published in May 2026; the final version has not yet been adopted.

## Related laws that matter

The AI Act is not the only law that protects you from unfair AI.

- **GDPR (data protection).** It applies alongside the AI Act. The EU Court of Justice ruled in the SCHUFA case (C-634/21, 7 December 2023) that an automated credit score that strongly determines a lender's decision counts as an automated decision under Article 22 GDPR. In Dun & Bradstreet Austria (C-203/22, 27 February 2025) it ruled that you have a right to a meaningful explanation of the procedure and principles behind an automated decision, so you can understand and contest it. These Article 22 rights apply to decisions made solely by automated means with legal or similarly significant effects. Proposed changes to GDPR, including Article 22, are still being negotiated; nothing in GDPR has changed yet.
- **Platform Work Directive (EU) 2024/2831.** EU countries must turn it into national law by **2 December 2026**. It gives people working through digital platforms rights on algorithmic management: transparency, human oversight and review of automated decisions, limits on processing worker data, and information and consultation. Many countries are running late.
- **Product Liability Directive (EU) 2024/2853.** It covers software, including AI, and applies to products placed on the market from **9 December 2026** (it is not retroactive). It makes it easier to claim compensation for harm caused by defective products. (A separate AI Liability Directive was withdrawn by the Commission in 2025.)
- **Council of Europe Framework Convention on Artificial Intelligence and Human Rights, Democracy and the Rule of Law.** The EU ratified this international treaty in 2026 and implements it through the AI Act.
