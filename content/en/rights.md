# Your AI Rights in Europe

**You have rights.** Whether it's an algorithm deciding your loan application, AI screening your job application, a chatbot answering your questions, or facial recognition in public spaces, European law gives you the power to understand, question, and challenge these systems.

This isn't about understanding complex regulations. It's about knowing what you can do when AI affects your life. Technology is a tool; the human factor is key, and that includes you.

## Your Four Key Rights

### 🔍 Right to Know
If a decision with serious effects on you is made entirely by an automated system, the organisation must tell you and explain the logic involved (GDPR). From 2 December 2027, organisations using high-risk AI to make decisions about you must also tell you. Since 2 August 2026, you also have the right to know when you're simply *dealing with* AI: chatbots must say they are AI, and deepfakes must be labelled.

[Learn how to spot AI and what must be labelled →](/en/rights/ai-transparency)

### 💡 Right to Explanation
When a decision with serious effects on you is made automatically, without meaningful human involvement, you're entitled to a meaningful explanation of how the decision was reached. And whenever AI is involved, you can always ask: *Why?* In 2025, the EU's highest court confirmed that this means a real, understandable explanation, not just a pile of technical documents.

[Learn more about your right to explanation →](/en/rights/right-to-explanation)

### 👤 Right to Human Review
You don't have to accept a purely automated decision. In many cases, you can request that a real person reviews the AI's decision.

### ✋ Right to Challenge
If you believe an automated decision is wrong or unfair, you can contest the decision and give your point of view (GDPR Art. 22(3), where it applies).

## Common Situations Where Your Rights Apply

AI touches more of our daily lives than most people realise. Here are situations where knowing your rights matters:

### At Work
From productivity monitoring to AI-assisted hiring, your workplace may use AI in ways that affect you. Some practices, like AI that tries to read your emotions at work or in a job interview, have been banned since February 2025.

[Understand your workplace AI rights →](/en/rights/workplace-surveillance)

### In Public Spaces
Facial recognition and biometric surveillance have strict limits in Europe. Know what's allowed and what's not.

[Learn about facial recognition rules →](/en/rights/facial-recognition)

### When Algorithms Decide
Applying for a loan? A job? Government benefits? Algorithms often play a role in these life-changing decisions.

[Know your rights with algorithmic decisions →](/en/rights/algorithmic-decisions)

### Online: Chatbots and Deepfakes
Is that customer service agent a person or a bot? Is that video real? New EU transparency rules mean you should be told.

[Know when you're dealing with AI →](/en/rights/ai-transparency)

### Fake Intimate Images
From 2 December 2026, AI tools that create fake nude or sexual images of real people without their consent (so-called "nudifier" apps) are banned in the EU. If this has happened to you, you are not alone and there are steps you can take.

[Read about the ban on intimate deepfakes →](/en/rights/intimate-deepfakes)

## What Can I Do Right Now?

If you think AI has been used in a decision that affects you:

**1. Ask if AI was involved**
You can always ask, and in many cases the organisation must tell you. Ask directly: "Was any automated system or algorithm involved in this decision?"

**2. Request an explanation**
If AI was used, ask for a clear explanation of how the decision was made and what factors were considered.

**3. Ask for human review**
If you're unhappy with an automated decision, request that a human reviews your case. This is especially important for significant decisions.

**4. Document everything**
Keep records of your communications and any decisions you receive. This helps if you need to challenge something later.

**5. Know where to complain**
If an organisation won't respect your rights, you can contact your national data protection authority or consumer protection agency. For AI Act issues, each EU country also has a "market surveillance authority" (the body that checks products and AI systems follow the rules). Some countries were slow to appoint theirs, so if you're unsure, start with your data protection authority.

## The Legal Foundation

Your rights come from two landmark pieces of European legislation:

**The EU AI Act** (2024) is widely described as the world's first comprehensive AI law. It is being phased in step by step:

- **Since February 2025:** a list of harmful AI practices is banned, such as social scoring and emotion recognition at work and in schools. Organisations using AI must also help their staff build "AI literacy" (a basic understanding of the AI tools they use).
- **Since August 2026:** transparency rules apply (chatbots must disclose they're AI, deepfakes must be labelled), and authorities can now enforce the law.
- **From 2 December 2026:** a new ban on AI that generates non-consensual intimate images or child sexual abuse material.
- **From 2 December 2027:** strict rules for "high-risk" AI used in areas like hiring, credit, education, benefits and policing. This date was moved back from August 2026 by an amending law adopted in 2026.
- **From 2 August 2028:** the same high-risk rules for AI built into products such as medical devices, toys and vehicles.

**The General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR)** (2018) gives you rights over your personal data, including the right to know about and challenge automated decisions. These GDPR rights apply today, regardless of the AI Act's timeline. Changes to the GDPR have been proposed but are still being negotiated; nothing has changed yet.

These laws work together to protect you. You don't need to understand every detail. Just know that the law gives you real tools.
