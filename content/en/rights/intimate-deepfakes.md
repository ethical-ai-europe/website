# Intimate Deepfakes: The New EU Ban

*This page talks about fake sexual images created with AI. It contains no images or graphic descriptions. If this has happened to you, please know: it is not your fault, and you are not alone.*

Some AI apps and websites let anyone upload an ordinary photo of a real person and turn it into a fake nude or sexual image. These are often called **"nudifier" apps**. The person in the photo never agreed, and often never even knows, until the image appears somewhere.

From **2 December 2026**, the EU is banning this.

## What the New Ban Covers

An amending law adopted in 2026 added a new item to the AI Act's list of **prohibited AI practices**, the uses of AI considered so harmful that they are simply not allowed in the EU. From 2 December 2026, the ban targets AI systems that:

- **Generate or manipulate realistic intimate or sexually explicit depictions of identifiable people without their consent.** This is the "nudifier" problem.
- **Generate child sexual abuse material.** There is no situation in which this is acceptable.

In plain terms:
- **Companies that provide these AI tools** break the ban where such images are the intended use, or a reasonably foreseeable one, and they have not put effective safeguards in place to prevent it.
- **Organisations that use an AI system** to generate such material also break the ban.

## What This Means for Victims

- **The law recognises the harm.** Creating fake intimate images of someone is not a joke or a "prank". EU law now treats AI that does this as one of the most harmful uses of the technology.
- **Companies can't hide behind "it's just a tool".** Companies that offer these apps, and organisations that use them, are responsible under the ban. Individuals who create or share such images are mainly dealt with by criminal law, which EU countries must strengthen by June 2027 under the EU directive on violence against women (Directive (EU) 2024/1385).
- **Serious penalties.** Breaking an AI Act ban can lead to fines of up to **€35 million or 7% of a company's worldwide annual turnover**, whichever is higher. For SMEs and start-ups, the lower of the two amounts applies.
- **Other laws protect you too.** Data protection law, criminal law and online platform rules in your country and across the EU may already cover this kind of abuse. The AI Act ban adds another layer; it doesn't replace your existing protections.

The ban starts on 2 December 2026, but that doesn't mean you have to wait to act. You can take steps today.

## What You Can Do Now

**1. Look after yourself first**
Finding a fake intimate image of yourself can be deeply upsetting. Reach out to someone you trust. Many countries have helplines and support organisations for victims of image-based abuse; your local police or a victim support service can point you to them.

**2. Keep evidence**
**If the person shown is under 18, skip this step: do not screenshot or save anything; go straight to step 6.**

Otherwise, before anything is taken down, if you feel able to, record where it appeared: save links, take screenshots of the page (including the username and date), and note when you found it. Store this somewhere private and safe. If this is too hard to do yourself, ask someone you trust to help.

**3. Report it to the platform**
Social media sites, messaging apps and websites usually have a reporting option for non-consensual intimate images. Use it, and keep a copy of your report.

**4. Report it to the police**
Creating or sharing intimate images without consent may be a crime in your country, whether or not they are fake. Police can also help with removal and investigation.

**5. Contact your data protection authority**
An image of you is personal data. Your national data protection authority can help if a company won't remove it or is processing your image unlawfully.

**6. If a child is involved**
Report it to the police immediately. Do not share, forward or save copies of the material yourself, even to report it: tell the police or platform where it is.

## What About AI That Isn't Banned?

Not every AI-generated image is banned, but since 2 August 2026, **deepfakes must be labelled** as artificially generated, and AI tools must mark their output in a machine-readable way. [Learn more about AI transparency rules →](/en/rights/ai-transparency)

## Who Enforces the Ban?

National authorities in each EU country enforce the AI Act's bans, and the EU AI Office supervises certain AI systems directly, including AI in very large online platforms. Some countries were late in appointing their authorities, so for now your fastest routes are usually the platform, the police and your data protection authority.
