# Your Right to an AI Explanation

You don't have to accept "the computer said no." When an automated system makes a decision with serious effects on you, you have the right to understand why. Even when AI only influenced the decision, you can ask, and you always have the right to see the personal data used about you. This guide shows you how to get that explanation.

## What This Right Means

Under European law, you have the right to **meaningful information about the logic involved** when a decision about you is made by automated means and significantly affects you. This isn't just a technical detail. It's about your ability to:

- **Understand** what happened and why
- **Check** if the decision was fair and correct
- **Challenge** outcomes that seem wrong
- **Learn** what you could do differently if appropriate

Think of it as the AI equivalent of getting your exam back with marks and comments, not just a grade.

## What Europe's Highest Court Has Confirmed

You don't have to take our word for it. The Court of Justice of the EU (CJEU), whose rulings apply in every EU country, has spelled this right out:

**Dun & Bradstreet Austria (case C-203/22, 27 February 2025)**
This case was about an automated credit assessment. The Court said you have the right to a **meaningful explanation of the procedure and principles** that were actually applied to your data, so that you can **understand the decision and contest it**. Handing you a complicated formula or the algorithm itself is *not* enough. The explanation has to make sense to you.

**SCHUFA (case C-634/21, 7 December 2023)**
The Court said that when an automated credit score **strongly determines** whether a lender says yes or no, calculating that score already counts as an "automated decision" under the GDPR. This means the organisation behind the score can't sidestep your rights just because someone else made the final call.

**What this means for you:** If an organisation replies with jargon, a wall of technical documents, or "the system decided," you can point to these rulings and ask again for a clear explanation.

## What You Can Expect to Get

A good explanation should tell you:

### The Basics
- ✅ **Confirmation that AI was used**
- ✅ **The type of decision or prediction made**
- ✅ **The outcome of the decision**

### The Reasoning
- ✅ **What factors the AI considered** (the inputs)
- ✅ **Which factors were most important** in your specific case
- ✅ **The general logic** of how the system works
- ✅ **Any thresholds or rules** that affected the outcome

### The Context
- ✅ **What data about you was used**
- ✅ **Where that data came from**
- ✅ **The consequences** of the decision for you

## What You Cannot Always Expect

Some things may not be provided:

- ❌ **The complete algorithm or source code** - This may be protected as a trade secret, though your right to explanation still applies
- ❌ **Real-time technical details** - The explanation should be understandable, not a data science report
- ❌ **A guarantee of what would change the outcome** - but they should explain how different data would have affected the result where possible
- ❌ **Perfect accuracy** - AI systems are probabilistic; explanations reflect this

**Important:** The law balances transparency with legitimate business interests, but your right to a meaningful explanation cannot be overridden by claims of trade secrets alone. And as the Dun & Bradstreet ruling shows, the reverse is also true: dumping source code on you doesn't count as an explanation either. Where an organisation claims trade secrets, the Court said the information can be given to the data protection authority or court, which then balances the interests (C-203/22).

## How to Request an Explanation

### Template Request

Send this to the organisation (email or letter):

---

**Subject: Request for Explanation of Automated Decision**

Dear [Organisation/Data Controller],

I am writing regarding a decision that was made about me on [date] concerning [describe: loan application, job application, benefits claim, etc.].

Under the General Data Protection Regulation (Articles 13, 14, 15, and 22), as interpreted by the Court of Justice of the EU in case C-203/22 (Dun & Bradstreet Austria), I request the following information:

1. **Confirmation** of whether automated decision-making or AI profiling was involved in this decision.

2. **Meaningful information about the logic involved**, including:
   - The main factors or criteria the system considered
   - Which factors were most significant in my specific case
   - How these factors led to the decision made about me

3. **The specific data about me** that was used in this decision and its source.

4. **The intended consequences** of this processing for me.

5. **My rights** regarding this decision, including how to request human review.

Please respond within one month as required by GDPR Article 12.

If any part of this request is unclear, please contact me for clarification rather than refusing to respond.

Yours faithfully,
[Your name]
[Your contact information]

---

### Tips for Effective Requests

**Be specific** - Reference the exact decision you're asking about

**Put it in writing** - Email or letter creates a record

**Use the law** - Mentioning the GDPR (and the Dun & Bradstreet ruling) signals you know your rights. Today the GDPR is your strongest tool for explanations; the AI Act adds more for high-risk systems from December 2027

**Set expectations** - The one-month deadline is legally required (it can be extended by two months for complex requests)

**Stay polite but firm** - You're exercising a right, not asking a favor

## What Makes a Good vs. Inadequate Explanation

### ✅ A Good Explanation

"Your loan application was declined based on our automated credit assessment. The main factors were:

1. **Credit history (high importance)** - Your credit file shows missed payments in the last 12 months
2. **Debt-to-income ratio (medium importance)** - Your existing debts are 45% of your income, above our threshold of 40%
3. **Employment stability (low importance)** - Your current employment of 8 months met our minimum requirement

The system is designed to predict repayment likelihood. Your overall score was 58, below our threshold of 65. You may request a human review or reapply after addressing the factors above."

**Why this works:** Specific, personal, actionable, with clear next steps.

### ❌ An Inadequate Explanation

"Your application was assessed by our system and declined based on our internal criteria. We cannot share details of our proprietary scoring methodology. This decision was made in accordance with our policies."

**Why this fails:** Vague, not personal, no actionable information, hides behind secrecy.

## What to Do If the Explanation Is Inadequate

### Push Back

> Thank you for your response. However, the explanation provided does not meet the requirements of GDPR Articles 13-15 and 22, which require "meaningful information about the logic involved." The Court of Justice of the EU confirmed in case C-203/22 (Dun & Bradstreet Austria, 27 February 2025) that this means an explanation of the procedure and principles actually applied, in a form that allows me to understand and contest the decision.
>
> The response you provided does not tell me:
> - Which specific factors affected my decision
> - How those factors led to the outcome
> - What I could do to achieve a different result
>
> Please provide a more detailed explanation within 14 days. If I do not receive an adequate response, I will file a complaint with [your national data protection authority].

*The 14 days is a reasonable deadline you set; the legal deadline remains one month.*

### Escalate

If they still don't provide adequate information:

**1. File a complaint with your data protection authority**
- Free to do in all EU countries
- They can investigate and order compliance
- Find your authority at edpb.europa.eu

**2. Contact sector regulators**
- Financial services: banking/insurance regulators
- Healthcare: health service regulators
- Employment: labour rights agencies

**3. Seek legal advice**
- Consumer protection lawyers
- Data protection specialists
- Civil liberties organisations

## The Right to Explanation in Practice

Remember:

📝 **Document everything** - Keep copies of your requests and their responses

⏰ **Know the timelines** - They have one month to respond, extendable to three for complex cases

🎯 **Be specific** - The more specific your request, the harder it is to dodge

💪 **Don't give up** - Inadequate responses are themselves potential violations

🤝 **You're not alone** - Data protection authorities exist to help you

## Quick Reference: Your Rights at a Glance

- **Know if AI was involved** → GDPR Art. 13, 14, 15
- **Meaningful explanation of logic** → GDPR Art. 13, 14, 15
- **Know data used about you** → GDPR Art. 15
- **Object to automated decisions** → GDPR Art. 22
- **Request human review** → GDPR Art. 22
- **Explanation you can actually understand and contest** → GDPR, confirmed by CJEU C-203/22
- **Know you're talking to a chatbot** → EU AI Act Art. 50 (since 2 August 2026)
- **Non-discrimination** → EU Charter of Fundamental Rights and equality law; AI Act data-quality and bias rules for high-risk AI from 2 December 2027
- **Explanation of high-risk AI decisions** → EU AI Act Art. 86 (from 2 December 2027)
- **Transparency from high-risk AI** → EU AI Act (from 2 December 2027)
- **Complain to authorities** → GDPR Art. 77; AI Act Art. 85 (complaint to market surveillance authority)

*Note: The GDPR rights above apply today and have not changed. Changes to the GDPR have been proposed but are still being negotiated by EU lawmakers.*
