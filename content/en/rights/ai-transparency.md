# Know When You're Dealing with AI

Is the friendly assistant on that website a person or a program? Is that video of a politician real? Was this news article written by a journalist or a machine? You deserve to know. Since **2 August 2026**, in many cases EU law says you should be told.

These transparency rules come from **Article 50 of the EU AI Act**. They are not "coming soon": they are in force now.

## Why This Matters

Trust is human. When we talk to someone, we adjust how much we share, how much we believe, and how we decide, based on who we think is on the other side. AI that pretends to be human, or fake content that looks real, takes that choice away from you. These rules are about giving it back.

## What the Rules Require

### 💬 Chatbots and AI assistants must say they're AI

If you're interacting with an AI system, such as a customer service chatbot, a voice assistant on a phone line, or an AI "agent" sending you messages, you must be informed that you're dealing with AI. The only exception is when it's already obvious to a reasonable person.

**In practice:** a clear message like "You're chatting with an AI assistant" at the start of the conversation.

### 🎭 Deepfakes must be labelled

A "deepfake" is an image, audio clip or video created or altered by AI to look or sound real, for example showing a real person saying or doing something they never did. Organisations and professionals who use AI to produce deepfakes must **disclose that the content is artificially generated or manipulated**. For obviously artistic, satirical or fictional work, the disclosure can be light-touch so it doesn't spoil the work. Purely private, non-professional use is not covered by the AI Act.

### 📰 AI-written public-interest texts must be labelled

When AI-generated text is published to inform the public about matters of public interest, such as news, politics or public health, it must be disclosed that the text was generated or manipulated by AI, unless a human has reviewed or edited it and a person or organisation (for example a newsroom) takes editorial responsibility for it.

### 🏷️ AI content must carry a hidden, machine-readable mark

Companies that provide generative AI tools (tools that create text, images, audio or video) must mark what their systems produce in a **machine-readable** way. Think of it as an invisible label that software, platforms and fact-checkers can detect, even when you can't see it.

**Grace period:** generative AI systems that were already on the market before 2 August 2026 have until **2 December 2026** to add this marking. After that, it applies to all of them.

### 🙂 You must be told about emotion recognition and biometric categorisation

If an organisation uses AI to recognise emotions or to sort people into categories based on their face, voice or body (where this is still allowed), it must **inform the people exposed to it**. Remember: emotion recognition at work and in schools, including job interviews, has been **banned** since February 2025 (except for medical or safety reasons).

[Read more about facial recognition rules →](/en/rights/facial-recognition)

## Who Must Follow These Rules?

- **Providers:** the companies that build AI tools (for example, the maker of a chatbot or image generator) must design them to disclose they are AI and to mark their output.
- **Deployers:** the organisations that use AI tools (for example, a bank using a chatbot, a marketing agency making deepfake ads, a shop using emotion analysis) must inform you and label content.

## What You Can Do If Something Isn't Labelled

**1. Ask directly**
If you're unsure whether you're talking to a human, ask: "Am I talking to a person or an AI?" The organisation should be transparent.

**2. Report it on the platform**
Most social media and video platforms let you report misleading or manipulated content. Use those tools, especially for deepfakes of real people.

**3. Keep a record**
Take screenshots, save links, note the date and time. This helps any complaint later.

**4. Complain to the right authority**
- For AI Act transparency breaches: your country's **market surveillance authority** (the national body that checks products and AI systems follow the rules). If you don't know who that is, your data protection authority or consumer body is a good first stop. The EU's [AI Act Service Desk](https://ai-act-service-desk.ec.europa.eu/) also has general information about the rules.
- If your personal data or image is involved: your national **data protection authority**.
- If a company misled you as a customer: your national **consumer protection** body.

**5. If a deepfake shows you in an intimate or sexual way**
This is serious, and it is not your fault. A specific ban on AI that creates such images applies from 2 December 2026. [Read our guide on intimate deepfakes →](/en/rights/intimate-deepfakes)

## Who Enforces These Rules?

- **National authorities** in each EU country supervise most organisations and can investigate complaints.
- The **EU AI Office** (part of the European Commission) directly supervises certain AI systems, including (under the 2026 amendment) AI built on a company's own general-purpose AI model and AI used in very large online platforms and search engines.

Enforcement powers have been live since 2 August 2026. Be aware that several EU countries were late in appointing their authorities, so in some places the system is still being set up. That doesn't remove your rights: it just means you may need to be patient, or start with your data protection or consumer authority.

## What Happens to Those Who Break the Rules?

Breaking the transparency rules can lead to fines of up to **€15 million or 3% of a company's worldwide annual turnover**, whichever is higher. For SMEs and "small mid-caps", the lower of the two amounts applies.

## Official Guidance

The European Commission published guidelines on these transparency rules in July 2026, and a code of practice on marking and labelling AI-generated content was also assessed that month. Guidelines aren't legally binding, but they show how authorities read the rules.
