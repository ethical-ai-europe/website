# When Algorithms Decide: Your Rights

Algorithms now influence some of life's biggest decisions: whether you get a loan, a job, benefits, or medical treatment. In many cases you have the right to know when algorithms are involved and to challenge decisions that affect you.

## Where Algorithms Make Decisions

You might not always see them, but algorithms are working behind the scenes in:

### Financial Services
- **Credit scoring** - Whether you're approved for a loan or mortgage
- **Insurance pricing** - What you pay for car, home, or health insurance
- **Fraud detection** - Whether your transaction is flagged or blocked
- **Investment advice** - Automated recommendations for your money

### Employment
- **Resume screening** - Whether your application reaches a human
- **Interview scheduling** - Which candidates get called back
- **Performance scoring** - How your work is evaluated
- **Promotion decisions** - Who gets considered for advancement

### Public Services
- **Benefits eligibility** - Whether you qualify for support
- **Social services** - Risk assessments and case prioritisation
- **Tax assessments** - Flagging returns for review
- **Public housing** - Allocation and priority decisions

### Healthcare
- **Diagnostic support** - AI-assisted medical diagnoses
- **Treatment recommendations** - Suggested care pathways
- **Resource allocation** - Prioritising appointments or procedures
- **Insurance approvals** - Pre-authorisation decisions

### Education
- **Admissions** - University and school applications
- **Grading assistance** - AI-supported assessment
- **Student monitoring** - Attendance and performance tracking
- **Proctoring** - AI-watched exams

## Your Legal Protections

Two major laws protect you:

### GDPR Article 22: Protection from Automated Decisions

You have the right **not to be subject to a decision based solely on automated processing** that significantly affects you. This means:

✓ Automated decisions need **human involvement** for significant outcomes
✓ You can request **human review** of automated decisions
✓ You're entitled to an **explanation** of the decision
✓ You can **contest** the decision and express your view

These rights apply in full when a decision is made solely by automated means and significantly affects you. Some exceptions apply, for example where a national law authorises the automated decision, but that law must then provide its own safeguards.

**This right applies today.** Changes to the GDPR, including to Article 22, have been proposed as part of a wider EU "simplification" package, but they are still being negotiated. As of November 2026, nothing in the GDPR has changed.

### What Europe's Highest Court Has Said

Two rulings from the Court of Justice of the EU (CJEU) make these rights stronger in practice:

- **SCHUFA (case C-634/21, December 2023):** This case was about credit scores that lenders rely on. The Court said that when an automated score *strongly determines* the final decision, producing the score itself counts as an automated decision under Article 22. In plain terms: an organisation can't escape the rules by saying "a human pressed the final button" if the algorithm really decided.
- **Dun & Bradstreet Austria (case C-203/22, February 2025):** This case concerned a person affected by an automated credit assessment. The Court said people are entitled to a meaningful explanation of the *procedure and principles* actually used, so they can understand and challenge the result. Simply handing over a complex formula or algorithm is not enough.

[Read more about your right to an explanation →](/en/rights/right-to-explanation)

### EU AI Act: High-Risk System Requirements

AI systems used in areas like credit scoring, hiring, education, access to public benefits and essential services are classified as **high-risk** and will have to:

✓ Be tested for **accuracy, robustness, and bias**
✓ Have **human oversight** built in
✓ Provide **transparency** about how they work
✓ Maintain **records** for accountability
✓ Be registered in an **EU database** (with extra registration duties for public bodies that use them)

**When do these rules apply?** The high-risk rules for these areas apply from **2 December 2027**. They were originally due in August 2026 but were postponed by an amending law adopted in 2026. For AI built into regulated products such as medical devices, the date is **2 August 2028**. Public authorities that already use high-risk AI have until 2 August 2030 to bring those systems into line.

**What already applies today:**
- Some AI practices have been **banned since February 2025**, such as social scoring and AI that exploits people's vulnerabilities.
- Since **2 August 2026**, transparency rules apply: for example, you must be told when you're talking to a chatbot. [Learn more →](/en/rights/ai-transparency)
- Your **GDPR rights** (explanation, human review, contesting a decision) apply right now, whatever the AI Act timeline says.

## When to Push Back: A Decision Tree

Ask yourself these questions:

**1. Was AI or an algorithm involved in this decision?**
- If you don't know, ask. In many cases you have the right to be told.

**2. Does the decision significantly affect you?**
- Employment, credit, housing, benefits, healthcare: these are significant.

**3. Was there meaningful human involvement?**
- A human rubber-stamping AI output doesn't count as real oversight.

**4. Does the outcome seem wrong or unfair?**
- Trust your judgment. If something feels off, it's worth questioning.

**If you answered yes to these, you likely have grounds to push back.**

## How to Exercise Your Rights

### Step 1: Request Information

Write to the organisation asking:

> I am writing to request information under GDPR Articles 13-15 and 22.
>
> Regarding the recent decision about [describe the decision], please tell me:
> 1. Was any automated decision-making or AI system involved in this decision?
> 2. If so, what is the logic involved in the automated processing?
> 3. What information was used to make this decision?
> 4. What is the significance and potential consequences of such processing for me?
>
> Please respond within one month as required by law.

### Step 2: Request Human Review

If you learn AI was involved:

> I am exercising my right under GDPR Article 22(3) to obtain human intervention in the decision regarding [describe the decision].
>
> I request that a qualified person reviews my case, taking into account the following points I wish to express:
>
> [List your concerns or additional information]
>
> I also request the opportunity to contest this decision.

(This right applies where the automated decision was based on a contract with you or your explicit consent; otherwise still ask: many organisations offer review.)

### Step 3: Challenge the Decision

If human review doesn't resolve the issue:

> I am not satisfied with the outcome of the human review of [the decision].
>
> I believe the decision is [incorrect/unfair/discriminatory] because:
>
> [Explain your reasoning]
>
> I request a formal review of this decision and ask that you explain how my concerns have been addressed.

### Step 4: Escalate If Necessary

If the organisation doesn't respond appropriately:

**File a complaint** with your national data protection authority
- They can investigate and order remedies
- Complaints are free to file

**Contact sector regulators** for industry-specific issues
- Financial services regulators for banking/insurance
- Healthcare regulators for medical decisions
- Labour courts or employment tribunals for workplace decisions

**Seek legal advice** for significant harm
- You may be entitled to compensation for damages

## What Makes a Good Response from an Organisation?

When you ask for information or human review, a good response should include:

✅ Clear confirmation of whether AI was involved
✅ Understandable explanation of how the system works
✅ The specific factors that influenced your decision
✅ Evidence that a real human reviewed your case
✅ A clear explanation of the outcome
✅ Information about further appeals

## Red Flags in Responses

Watch out for:

🚩 **Refusal to confirm AI use** - If the decision was automated, they must tell you
🚩 **Vague explanations** - "The system decided" isn't enough
🚩 **No human involvement** - Rubber-stamping doesn't count
🚩 **No appeal option** - You should have recourse
🚩 **Ignoring your concerns** - They must consider what you say

## What a Successful Challenge Can Look Like

The first three examples below are illustrative, based on the kinds of outcomes the law makes possible. The court cases are real.

**Credit decision reversed** - A person denied credit asked for explanation, discovered an error in data used, and had the decision reversed after human review.

**Benefits reinstated** - An automated benefits cut was reversed when the individual showed the algorithm didn't account for their circumstances.

**Job application reconsidered** - A screened-out candidate requested review, and was interviewed after showing the AI missed relevant qualifications.

**Court cases that changed the rules** - In the SCHUFA and Dun & Bradstreet cases, complaints by ordinary people led national courts to ask the EU's highest court for guidance, and the answers gave everyone clearer rights.

**These outcomes are possible because of your rights.**
