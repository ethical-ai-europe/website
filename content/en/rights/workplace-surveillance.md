# AI Surveillance at Work: Your Rights

Your employer may be using AI to monitor your work, but that doesn't mean anything goes. European law sets clear limits on workplace surveillance, and some practices are banned entirely.

## What Counts as AI Surveillance at Work?

AI workplace monitoring comes in many forms. You might encounter:

- **Keystroke and mouse tracking** - Software that records how you use your computer
- **Productivity scoring** - AI that grades your performance based on activity
- **Screen monitoring** - Tools that capture or watch your screen
- **Email and chat analysis** - AI that reads your communications
- **Video surveillance** - Cameras that may use facial recognition or behaviour analysis
- **Emotion detection** - Systems that claim to read your mood or engagement
- **Location tracking** - GPS or network-based tracking of your movements
- **Time and attendance AI** - Automated systems that monitor when and how long you work

## What's Allowed, Restricted, and Banned

Here's a clear breakdown of where your rights stand:

### ❌ Prohibited (Banned Under EU AI Act)

These practices have been **banned** in European workplaces since **2 February 2025**:

- **Emotion recognition in the workplace** - Systems that infer your emotional state, feelings, or intentions from your face, voice or other body data while you work. This **includes recruitment**: AI that claims to read your emotions during a video job interview is banned too. Official EU guidance reads "workplace" broadly, so job candidates are covered. The only exceptions are for genuine medical or safety reasons
- **Biometric categorisation by sensitive attributes** - Systems that use your biometric data to infer race, political opinions, trade union membership, religious or philosophical beliefs, or sex life or sexual orientation
- **Social scoring** - Systems that score people on their social behaviour or personal characteristics, where the score leads to unfair treatment in an unrelated context or treatment out of proportion to the behaviour. Normal performance evaluation is not automatically social scoring, but it will be high-risk from 2 December 2027.

Employers that use a banned practice risk fines of up to **€35 million or 7% of worldwide annual turnover**, whichever is higher. For SMEs and start-ups, the lower of the two amounts applies. National authorities have had the power to enforce these bans since August 2026.

### ⚠️ Restricted (Requires Justification & Safeguards)

These will be classified as **high-risk AI systems** from 2 December 2027 and will have to meet strict requirements:

- **AI-assisted hiring and recruitment** - Must be transparent, tested for bias, and allow human oversight
- **Performance evaluation AI** - Must provide explanations and human review options
- **Promotion and termination decisions** - Under the GDPR, generally may not be left entirely to an automated system
- **Task allocation based on AI** - Must be fair and non-discriminatory

Employers using these systems will have to:
- Inform you that AI is being used
- Provide access to human decision-makers
- Maintain documentation and conduct regular testing

**When do these rules apply?** The AI Act's specific obligations for high-risk AI in employment and HR apply from **2 December 2027**. They were originally due in August 2026 but were postponed by an amending law adopted in 2026. **That doesn't leave you unprotected in the meantime:** the GDPR already gives you the right to know what data is collected, to get an explanation, and to ask for human review of significant automated decisions (GDPR Article 22). Those rights apply today and have not changed. Proposed changes to the GDPR are still being negotiated.

### ✅ Generally Allowed (With Transparency)

- **Basic time tracking** - Recording work hours
- **Standard productivity tools** - Email, collaboration software, project management
- **Network security monitoring** - For legitimate security purposes
- **Anonymised analytics** - Aggregate data about workplace patterns

Even these require transparency: you should be informed about what data is collected.

### 📚 AI Literacy: Your Employer's Duty to Help You Understand

Since February 2025, every organisation that uses AI, of any size, must take steps to support the "AI literacy" of its staff. That means helping people who work with AI tools understand what those tools can and can't do. The rule was reworded in 2026: employers must take measures to support your AI literacy, but they don't have to guarantee a specific skill level for each person. If you're asked to use AI at work without any guidance, it's fair to ask for training.

## Working for a Platform? Extra Rights Are Coming

If you work through an app or digital platform (for example delivery, ride-hailing or online task work), the EU **Platform Work Directive** gives you extra rights about "algorithmic management", meaning when an app assigns, rates or deactivates you:

- **Transparency** about how automated systems monitor you and make decisions
- **Human oversight** of those systems
- **Human review** of important automated decisions, so you can challenge them
- **Limits** on what personal data the platform may process about you
- **Information and consultation** for workers and their representatives

EU countries must turn this directive into national law by **2 December 2026**. Many countries are running late, so check with a union or worker organisation about the situation where you live.

## Know the Red Flags

Watch out for these warning signs:

🚩 **No clear policy** - If your employer can't explain what monitoring is in place, that's a problem

🚩 **Constant surveillance** - Being watched every second, including breaks

🚩 **Emotion or mood tracking** - Claims to detect your stress levels, engagement, or feelings

🚩 **No human oversight** - AI making decisions about you with no way to appeal

🚩 **Secret monitoring** - Surveillance you weren't told about

🚩 **Punitive scoring** - Arbitrary productivity scores that affect your job security

## Your Rights in Action

### Step 1: Find Out What's Being Used

Ask your employer (in writing):
- What monitoring or AI systems are used in the workplace?
- What data is collected about employees?
- How is this data used in decisions about employment, performance, or discipline?
- Who has access to this data?

For the data about you, this is a GDPR access request and they must respond within one month.

### Step 2: Review Your Employment Documents

Check your:
- Employment contract
- Employee handbook
- IT and monitoring policies
- Data protection notices

These should disclose monitoring practices. If they don't match what's actually happening, that's a concern.

### Step 3: Document Your Concerns

If you believe your rights are being violated:
- Keep a written record of what you observe
- Save any communications about monitoring
- Note dates and specifics

### Step 4: Take Action

**Talk to HR or management** - Raise concerns formally, in writing

**Contact your works council or union** - They often have rights to be consulted on workplace monitoring, and colleagues raising concerns together are usually heard more clearly than one person alone

**File a complaint** - Your national data protection authority can investigate violations

**Seek legal advice** - Employment lawyers can advise on your specific situation

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can my employer read my emails?

Work email accounts may be monitored, but employers should tell you this in advance. Personal communications have stronger protections. Using AI to analyse the content of your messages for emotional state or similar purposes is restricted, and AI that reads your emotions from your face or voice at work is banned (except for medical or safety reasons).

### What if I work remotely?

The same rules apply whether you work from home or the office. Remote monitoring software must comply with the same standards. You should be informed about any monitoring tools on work devices.

### Can AI analyse my face or voice in a job interview?

Not to read your emotions. Since February 2025, AI that claims to detect emotions such as nervousness or enthusiasm from your face or voice is banned in recruitment as well as at work. If you suspect this happened, ask the employer in writing what tools were used and contact your data protection authority.

### Can AI be used to decide if I get promoted or fired?

AI can assist in these decisions, but in most cases there must be meaningful human involvement for significant outcomes. You have the right to an explanation and, in many cases, to request human review. [Read about your right to an explanation →](/en/rights/right-to-explanation)

### What if my employer is based outside the EU?

If you work in the EU, EU data protection and AI rules generally apply to monitoring of you, even if your employer is headquartered elsewhere.
