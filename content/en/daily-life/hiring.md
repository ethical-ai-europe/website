# AI in Hiring & Recruitment

When you apply for a job today, there's a good chance AI is involved—from screening your resume to analyzing your video interview. For job seekers and HR professionals alike, understanding these systems is essential.

## How AI Is Used in Hiring

### CV and Resume Screening
AI parses applications to filter candidates based on keywords, qualifications, or predicted fit. Many applications never reach human eyes.

### Video Interview Analysis
AI analyzes recorded interviews, assessing word choice, tone of voice, or body language to score candidates. Note: using AI to infer a candidate's **emotions** from their face or voice has been banned in the EU since February 2025 (see below).

### Skills Assessments
AI-powered tests evaluate technical abilities, cognitive skills, or personality traits, often as a first-round filter.

### Candidate Ranking and Matching
Algorithms rank candidates by predicted success or fit, determining who advances to interviews.

### Chatbots and Automated Communication
AI handles initial candidate questions, schedules interviews, and sends updates—often without human involvement.

### Automated Rejections
AI can send rejection notices without human review, especially at early stages.

### Reference and Background Checks
AI aggregates information from databases, social media, or other sources about candidates.

## The Problem: Documented Bias Issues

AI hiring tools have been repeatedly shown to perpetuate or amplify discrimination:

### Known Issues
- **Amazon's experimental recruiting tool** (abandoned by 2017 and revealed by Reuters in 2018) learned to penalise résumés that included the word "women's"
- **Facial analysis systems** have shown bias against people with darker skin and non-Western facial features
- **Language models** may penalize non-native speakers or regional dialects
- **Historical hiring data** often encodes past discrimination, which AI then learns and repeats
- **Keyword matching** can exclude qualified candidates who describe their experience differently

### Who Is Affected
Bias in AI hiring can disproportionately impact:
- Women
- Racial and ethnic minorities
- People with disabilities
- Older workers
- Non-native speakers
- Career changers or those with non-traditional paths

The efficiency AI promises can come at the cost of fairness.

## What the Law Says Today (November 2026)

The EU AI Act is the European Union's law on artificial intelligence. It entered into force in 2024 and its rules apply in stages. In July 2026 the EU postponed some of its dates. Here is what that means for job seekers.

### Already in force

✓ **Emotion recognition is banned at work and in recruitment** (since February 2025). Employers may not use AI that claims to read your emotions, for example from your face or voice in a video interview. Official EU guidance makes clear this protects job candidates too, not only employees. The only exceptions are narrow medical or safety reasons.

✓ **Chatbots must say they are AI** (since 2 August 2026). If a recruitment chatbot talks to you, it must make clear it is a machine, unless that is already obvious. See [When AI must tell you it's AI](/en/rights/ai-transparency).

✓ **AI literacy** (since February 2025). Employers using AI must take steps to help their HR staff understand it, including its limits.

✓ **GDPR and anti-discrimination law.** If a significant decision about you is made solely by a machine, your rights to be told, to get a meaningful explanation and to ask for human review apply now. So does the ban on discrimination, whatever tools are used.

### Delayed: "high-risk" rules for hiring AI

Under the AI Act, AI systems used in employment are classified as **high-risk**, including:

- Recruitment and selection (placing targeted job advertisements, screening, filtering, evaluating candidates)
- Decisions on promotion and termination
- Task allocation based on individual behaviour or personal traits, and performance monitoring
- Evaluating work performance

These requirements were originally due in August 2026. They now apply from **2 December 2027**. When they apply, they will require:

✓ **Transparency**: Employers must inform candidates and workers that high-risk AI is used

✓ **Human oversight**: Trained people must be able to check and overrule the AI

✓ **Bias testing**: Systems must be tested for discriminatory effects

✓ **Documentation**: Records of how systems work and have been validated

✓ **Accuracy**: Systems must meet quality and reliability standards

✓ **Data quality**: Training data must be relevant and representative

**What this means for you:** until December 2027 employers have fewer formal AI Act duties for hiring tools. That does not mean anything goes. The GDPR, equality law and the bans above protect you today. And you can always ask for a person to look at your application.

### Platform workers: new rights on algorithmic management

If you work through a digital platform (for example delivery or ride-hailing apps), the EU Platform Work Directive gives you rights when algorithms manage your work: transparency about how automated systems are used, human oversight and review of important automated decisions, and limits on what data can be collected about you. EU countries must put these rules into national law by **2 December 2026**. Many countries are running late, so check what applies where you live.

## Your Rights as a Candidate

### Right to Know AI Is Being Used

Under the GDPR, you have the right to be told if a decision with significant effects on you, such as rejecting your application, is made solely by automated means. You can also always ask what personal data the employer holds about you, and whether AI was used at all. If a recruitment chatbot talks to you, it must tell you it is AI.

### Right to Explanation If Rejected

If an automated system alone decided to reject your application, you have the right to a meaningful explanation. In the 2025 *Dun & Bradstreet Austria* case (C-203/22), the EU Court of Justice said this means explaining the procedure and principles actually used in a way you can understand and contest. Simply handing over a complex algorithm is not enough.

### Right to Human Review

Under GDPR Article 22, if a significant decision about you was made solely by a machine, you can ask for a human to step in, give your point of view, and contest the decision. This is especially important if you've been rejected at early stages without human involvement.

### Right to Object and to Complain

In some cases you can object to the processing of your personal data (GDPR Article 21), for example when an employer relies on its "legitimate interests". Separately, you can always complain to the employer, to your data protection authority or to an equality body if you believe AI treated you unfairly.

### Right to Non-Discrimination

AI cannot lawfully discriminate based on protected characteristics—even if it does so indirectly through proxies like name, address, or graduation year.

### Right to Your Data

You can request information about what data was processed about you during recruitment.

## Practical Tips for Job Seekers

### How to Ask If AI Was Used

You can inquire at any point in the process:

**Before applying:**
> "Does your recruitment process use any automated screening, AI analysis, or algorithmic decision-making tools?"

**After rejection:**
> "I would like to understand how my application was evaluated. Was any automated system or AI involved in the decision? I am requesting this information under GDPR Article 15."

**When invited to video interview:**
> "Will this video interview be analyzed by AI? What aspects are assessed, and how?"

### How to Request Human Review

If you've been rejected and believe AI was involved:

> "I am writing regarding my recent application for [position].
>
> I understand that automated screening or AI may have been involved in evaluating my application. If the decision was made solely by automated means, I am entitled under GDPR Article 22 to human intervention. In any case, I request human review of my application.
>
> I believe I am qualified for this role based on [briefly explain]. I ask that a hiring manager reviews my application directly, taking into account the points above.
>
> Please confirm receipt and let me know the outcome of this review."

### Template Language for Candidates

**Requesting information about AI use:**

> Subject: GDPR Information Request - Recruitment Process
>
> Dear [Company],
>
> I recently applied for / participated in the recruitment process for [position]. Under GDPR Articles 13-15 and 22, I request the following information:
>
> 1. Was any automated decision-making, AI, or algorithmic system used to evaluate my application?
> 2. If so, what is the logic involved in this automated processing?
> 3. What information about me was considered?
> 4. What is the significance and intended consequences of such processing?
>
> Please respond within one month as required by law (extendable by up to two further months in complex cases).

**Tip:** If the reply is vague, you can point out that the EU Court of Justice has confirmed your right to a meaningful explanation of the procedure and principles actually applied (Case C-203/22, 2025).

**Challenging a decision:**

> Subject: Request for Human Review of Application Decision
>
> Dear [Company],
>
> I recently received a rejection for [position]. I am exercising my right under GDPR Article 22 to request human intervention in this decision.
>
> I ask that a qualified person reviews my application, considering that [explain your qualifications or concerns].
>
> I also request the opportunity to understand and contest this decision.
>
> Please confirm you have received this request and advise on next steps.

## For Employers: Compliance Obligations

If you use AI in hiring, some duties apply today and others from 2 December 2027:

**Already applies:** no emotion recognition of candidates or staff; AI literacy measures for staff; chatbots must disclose they are AI; GDPR (including Article 22 on automated decisions) and equality law.

**From 2 December 2027:** the full AI Act high-risk duties for employment AI. Good practice is to prepare now:

### Before Deployment
- **Conduct risk assessments** for any AI hiring tools
- **Test for bias** against protected groups before use
- **Document** how systems work and what data they use
- **Train staff** who interact with or oversee AI systems (AI literacy is already required)

### During Use
- **Inform candidates** that AI is being used and how
- **Ensure human oversight** for consequential decisions
- **Maintain records** of AI-assisted decisions
- **Monitor for problems** and unexpected patterns

### Bias Checks
From 2 December 2027, providers of high-risk AI (the companies that make the tools) must examine their training data for possible biases and meet accuracy requirements. As the employer, you must use the system as instructed, monitor it, and inform workers' representatives and affected workers before using it at work.

Good practice is to ask vendors for evidence of testing across demographic groups and to keep checking outcomes yourself. Checking should be ongoing, not one-time.

### When Candidates Exercise Rights
- Respond to information requests within one month (extendable by up to two further months in complex cases)
- Provide meaningful explanations, not boilerplate
- Actually conduct human review when requested—not rubber-stamping
- Take objections and complaints seriously

### Consequences of Non-Compliance
Under the EU AI Act, maximum penalties are:
- Up to €35 million or 7% of global annual turnover (whichever is higher) for banned practices, such as emotion recognition of candidates
- Up to €15 million or 3% for most other breaches, including transparency duties and, once they apply, high-risk duties
- For small and medium-sized companies, the lower of the two amounts applies

National authorities can also order you to stop using a non-compliant system. GDPR fines and discrimination claims are separate and apply already. Beyond fines, there is reputational damage and the loss of good candidates.

## Red Flags for Job Seekers

🚩 **No disclosure**: No mention of AI in recruitment process

🚩 **Instant rejection**: Rejection within hours of applying (suggests automated screening)

🚩 **Video interview emphasis**: Heavy focus on recorded video with no human interaction

🚩 **"Emotion" or "enthusiasm" scores**: Any tool claiming to read your emotions from your face or voice. This is banned in recruitment in the EU

🚩 **Personality tests**: Extensive personality assessments without clear relevance

🚩 **Vague feedback**: "Not a fit" without explanation

🚩 **No human contact**: Inability to reach an actual person in the process

🚩 **Unusual data requests**: Requests for information that seems irrelevant

## Red Flags for Employers

🚩 **Vendor promises**: Claims of perfect accuracy or zero bias

🚩 **Emotion or personality-from-face features**: Emotion recognition of candidates is banned; switch these features off or choose another tool

🚩 **Black box systems**: Inability to explain how a tool makes decisions

🚩 **No validation**: Vendor cannot provide bias testing for your population

🚩 **Excessive efficiency**: Tool that rejects vast majority of candidates

🚩 **No human review**: Process designed with no human touchpoints

🚩 **Complaints emerging**: Candidates raising concerns about fairness

## What You Can Do

**Job seekers:**
- Ask about AI use in every application process
- Ask to speak to a person at any stage: the human factor still matters most in hiring
- Request human review if rejected without explanation
- Document your experiences and patterns you observe
- Report concerns to data protection authorities
- Support organizations advocating for fair hiring

**HR professionals and hiring managers:**
- Evaluate the AI tools your organization uses
- Push for transparency, testing, and human oversight
- Don't let AI become an excuse to avoid difficult decisions
- Stay informed about evolving regulations
- Prioritize fairness over efficiency

**Everyone:**
- Share experiences with AI in hiring
- Support research into AI hiring bias
- Advocate for stronger regulations and enforcement
- Recognize that fair hiring benefits everyone
