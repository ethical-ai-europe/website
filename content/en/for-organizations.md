# AI Guidance for Organisations

**Responsible AI isn't just ethical. It's good business.** Organisations that use AI thoughtfully build trust with customers, employees and regulators. They reduce legal risk, avoid costly mistakes and are better prepared for a regulated environment.

Whether you're a small business using AI tools, a school using educational technology, or a government agency serving citizens, this guide helps you understand your responsibilities under the EU AI Act.

> **What changed in 2026?** The Digital Omnibus on AI (Regulation (EU) 2026/1744), in force since 27 July 2026, moved the deadlines for most high-risk AI rules: to **2 December 2027** for high-risk uses such as hiring, credit and education, and to **2 August 2028** for AI built into regulated products. **The deadline moved, the direction didn't.** The bans, AI literacy and the transparency rules already apply today.

## What Applies Right Now (November 2026)

Every organisation that uses AI in the EU, whatever its size, should already be doing these things:

- **No prohibited AI.** Bans such as social scoring, manipulative AI and emotion recognition at work or in education have applied since February 2025.
- **AI literacy.** Since 27 July 2026 the rule (Article 4) says organisations must *take measures to support* the AI literacy of their staff. You don't have to guarantee a specific level of knowledge for each person, but you do need to act: training, guidance and clear rules for use. This applies to all organisations using AI, at any risk level. If you want outside help, look for hands-on training that puts people first; one example is [aitrainer.dk](https://aitrainer.dk), run by the founder of this site.
- **Transparency (Article 50)**, since 2 August 2026:
  - Chatbots and other AI that talks with people must make clear that it is AI (this is the provider's duty to build in; if you use a vendor's chatbot, check that it does).
  - Deepfakes, and AI-generated text published to inform the public on matters of public interest, must be labelled as AI-generated.
  - If you use emotion recognition or biometric categorisation (sorting people into groups based on body data), you must inform the people exposed to it.
  - Providers of generative AI must mark output in a machine-readable way. Systems already on the market before 2 August 2026 have until **2 December 2026**.
- **A new ban from 2 December 2026:** AI that creates realistic intimate or sexually explicit images of identifiable people without consent, including child sexual abuse material ("nudifier" apps). See [Intimate deepfakes](/en/rights/intimate-deepfakes).

Read more about labelling and disclosure in [AI transparency: your rights](/en/rights/ai-transparency).

## Quick Self-Assessment: What Kind of AI User Are You?

The AI Act distinguishes between **providers** (those who build an AI system or put it on the market under their name) and **deployers** (organisations that use an AI system in their work). Most organisations are deployers.

| Your Situation | Typical Obligations | Start Here |
|----------------|---------------------|------------|
| Using general AI tools (chatbots, AI assistants) for internal tasks | AI literacy, no prohibited uses, good practice | [SME Guide](/en/for-organizations/sme-guide) |
| AI in customer-facing products or services | Transparency obligations apply now (disclosure, labelling) | [SME Guide](/en/for-organizations/sme-guide) |
| Building or customising AI systems | Provider obligations; marking of generative AI output; high-risk rules if applicable | [Compliance Checklist](/en/for-organizations/compliance-checklist) |
| Using AI for HR, credit or other high-stakes decisions | Likely high-risk; full obligations from 2 Dec 2027, prepare now | [Compliance Checklist](/en/for-organizations/compliance-checklist) |
| AI in education (admissions, grading, proctoring) | Likely high-risk from 2 Dec 2027; emotion recognition ban applies now | [Educators Guide](/en/for-organizations/educators) |
| Government AI affecting citizens | If high-risk: high-risk rules, impact assessment and registration from 2 Dec 2027; existing systems by 2 Aug 2030 at the latest | [Public Sector Guide](/en/for-organizations/public-sector) |

## Ethics and Compliance: Two Sides of the Same Coin

Some organisations treat ethical AI and regulatory compliance as separate concerns: one for the philosophy department, one for the lawyers. **That's a mistake.**

The EU AI Act is built on ethical foundations:

- **Transparency** requirements exist because people deserve to know when AI affects them
- **Human oversight** requirements exist because algorithms shouldn't make life-changing decisions alone
- **Bias testing** requirements exist because AI shouldn't discriminate

When you build ethical AI systems, you're already doing most of what compliance requires. And when you follow compliance requirements, you're putting AI ethics into practice.

**The practical upshot:** Don't run separate "ethics" and "compliance" initiatives. Build one programme that does both. Human oversight, good documentation and trained people are good practice today, regardless of legal deadlines.

## Common Questions

### "We're small. Does the EU AI Act even apply to us?"

Yes, if you use AI systems that affect people in the EU. Size doesn't exempt you, but obligations scale with risk. SMEs and the new category of "small mid-caps" get some relief: lower maximum fines (for small mid-caps, not for banned practices) and priority access to regulatory sandboxes (supervised testing environments). Most SMEs using standard AI tools mainly need AI literacy measures, transparency where they face customers, and no prohibited uses.

### "We just use tools. We're not building AI."

That's the situation for most organisations. As a deployer you still have responsibilities, but they're lighter than for providers. The key is knowing which tools you use and what you use them for, because the *use* often decides the risk level.

### "The high-risk deadline moved. Can we relax?"

No. The delay to December 2027 exists because technical standards and guidance weren't ready, not because the rules became less important. Use the extra time to prepare properly: inventory, classify, ask vendors for documentation, and train people. Customers, employees and data protection authorities already expect responsible AI, and the GDPR applies in full today.

### "This seems complicated. Where do we start?"

Start with an inventory. What AI tools and systems does your organisation use, and for what? That simple question, answered honestly, is the foundation for everything else.

## Key Timelines

The EU AI Act is being phased in. Dates below reflect the 2026 amendments.

| Date | What Applies |
|------|-----------------|
| **1 August 2024** | Act enters into force |
| **2 February 2025** | Prohibited AI practices banned; AI literacy duty |
| **2 August 2025** | Rules for general-purpose AI (GPAI) models; governance and penalties |
| **27 July 2026** | Digital Omnibus in force; new AI literacy wording (Art. 4) and bias-detection data rule (Art. 4a) |
| **2 August 2026** | Transparency obligations (Art. 50); authorities supervise and enforce |
| **2 December 2026** | New ban on non-consensual intimate deepfakes; machine-readable marking deadline for existing generative AI |
| **2 August 2027** | Every Member State must have at least one AI regulatory sandbox running |
| **2 December 2027** | High-risk rules for Annex III uses (HR, credit, education, essential services, biometrics and more) |
| **2 August 2028** | High-risk rules for AI in regulated products (Annex I, e.g. medical devices, toys, lifts, vehicles) |
| **2 August 2030** | Deadline for high-risk AI already used by public authorities |

See the full [EU AI Act timeline](/en/timeline).

**Don't wait for the deadlines.** Organisations that prepare now will have an easier transition, and better AI in the meantime.

## Get Help

Understanding your obligations is one thing; implementing them is another. Here's where to find help:

- **[AI Act Service Desk](https://ai-act-service-desk.ec.europa.eu/)**: the European Commission's single information platform for the Act
- **Official guidance**: the Commission has published non-binding guidelines on prohibited practices, the definition of an AI system and the Article 50 transparency rules (July 2026). Draft guidelines on classifying high-risk AI were published in May 2026; the final version is still pending.
- **Standards**: the first harmonised standard (an official European technical standard; following it is a recognised way to show you meet the rules), EN 18286, on quality management systems, is available. Others (risk management, data, logging, accuracy, cybersecurity) are still being developed.
- **National AI authorities**: many Member States were late in designating them, so check what exists in your country. In Denmark, the Danish Agency for Digital Government (Digitaliseringsstyrelsen) is the single point of contact, and the Danish Data Protection Agency (Datatilsynet) supervises certain bans.
- **Industry associations and consultants**: useful for sector-specific or complex situations.
